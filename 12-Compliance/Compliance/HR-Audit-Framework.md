# HR Audit Framework

## **Purpose**

An HR audit provides a structured review of HR processes, documentation, controls, and practices to identify strengths, gaps, and potential risks.

---

## Audit Lifecycle

```text
Audit Scope
    ↓
Document Review
    ↓
Data Review
    ↓
Interviews
    ↓
Control Testing
    ↓
Findings
    ↓
Risk Assessment
    ↓
Corrective Action
    ↓
Follow-Up
```

---

## 1. Audit Scope

Possible areas:

* Recruitment
* Employee records
* Onboarding
* Payroll controls
* Attendance
* Leave
* Performance
* Employee relations
* Learning
* Policies
* Data protection

---

## 2. Evidence

Potential evidence includes:

* Policies
* Contracts
* HRIS records
* Employee files
* Approval records
* Training records
* Payroll reports
* Employee relations documentation

---

## 3. Audit Finding

Each finding should identify:

* Requirement
* Current condition
* Evidence
* Gap
* Risk
* Recommended action
* Responsible owner
* Deadline

---

## 4. Risk Rating

| Rating   | Meaning                        |
| -------- | ------------------------------ |
| Low      | Limited risk                   |
| Moderate | Requires corrective action     |
| High     | Significant potential exposure |
| Critical | Requires immediate escalation  |

---

## 5. Corrective Action Plan

| Finding               | Action                 | Owner | Deadline | Status |
| --------------------- | ---------------------- | ----- | -------- | ------ |
| Missing records       | Complete documentation | HR    | 30 days  | Open   |
| Policy gap            | Review policy          | HR    | 45 days  | Open   |
| Process inconsistency | Standardize process    | HRBP  | 30 days  | Open   |

---

## 6. Follow-Up

The audit should not end with the report.

Follow-up should confirm:

* Action completed
* Evidence available
* Risk reduced
* Control operating effectively

---

## HRBP Role

The HRBP can use audit findings to identify recurring organizational weaknesses and recommend improvements.
