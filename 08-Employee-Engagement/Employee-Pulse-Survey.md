# Employee Pulse Survey

## 1. Purpose

The Employee Pulse Survey is a short, focused employee-listening tool designed to monitor changes in employee experience and engagement between comprehensive engagement surveys.

Unlike an annual engagement survey, a pulse survey focuses on a smaller number of targeted questions and can be used to understand specific workplace issues over time.

---

## 2. Objectives

The pulse survey can be used to:

* Monitor employee sentiment
* Identify emerging workplace concerns
* Measure changes after an HR intervention
* Gather feedback on specific organizational issues
* Support manager effectiveness
* Monitor communication
* Assess employee experience
* Identify areas requiring further investigation

---

## 3. Recommended Use

A pulse survey should have a clear purpose before it is launched.

Examples include:

* Measuring employee experience after organizational change
* Assessing communication effectiveness
* Evaluating a new manager initiative
* Checking employee morale
* Understanding workload concerns
* Measuring recognition
* Evaluating a new workplace policy
* Checking progress after an engagement action plan

The organization should avoid conducting surveys without a clear plan for reviewing and responding to the results.

# 4. Survey Rating Scale

Unless otherwise stated, use:

| Score | Response                   |
| ----: | -------------------------- |
|     1 | Strongly Disagree          |
|     2 | Disagree                   |
|     3 | Neither Agree nor Disagree |
|     4 | Agree                      |
|     5 | Strongly Agree             |

# 5. Core Pulse Questions

## Leadership

1. I understand the organization's current priorities.

2. Leadership communicates important changes effectively.

## Manager Effectiveness

3. My manager provides the support I need to perform effectively.

4. I receive useful feedback from my manager.

## Communication

5. I receive the information I need to do my work effectively.

6. I understand how recent organizational changes affect my role.

## Recognition

7. My contributions are appropriately recognized.

## Career Development

8. I have opportunities to develop my skills.

9. I understand what I can do to progress in my career.

## Workplace Experience

10. I have the resources I need to perform my job effectively.

11. I feel respected in my workplace.

## Engagement

12. I feel motivated to contribute to the organization's success.

13. I would recommend this organization as a good place to work.

14. I intend to continue working here.

# 6. Open-Ended Questions

### Question 15

**What is one thing we are doing well?**

### Question 16

**What is one thing we should improve?**

### Question 17

**Is there anything else you would like leadership to know?**

Open-ended questions should be reviewed for recurring themes rather than focusing unnecessarily on individual comments.

# 8. Survey Administration Process

## Step 1: Define Objective

Clearly state what the survey is intended to measure.

## Step 2: Select Questions

Use only questions relevant to the objective.

## Step 3: Confirm Confidentiality

Determine how responses will be protected and reported.

## Step 4: Launch Survey

Distribute the survey through an appropriate platform.

## Step 5: Monitor Participation

Track response rates without compromising employee confidentiality.

## Step 6: Close Survey

End the survey after the agreed response period.

## Step 7: Analyze Results

Identify overall trends and meaningful differences.

## Step 8: Identify Actions

Determine whether intervention is required.

## Step 9: Communicate Findings

Share appropriate high-level findings with employees and leaders.

## Step 10: Follow Up

Measure whether agreed actions produced improvement.

# 11. Response Rate

Response rate can be calculated as:

**Response Rate = Number of Completed Responses ÷ Number of Employees Invited × 100**

Example:

If 180 employees are invited and 144 complete the survey:

**144 ÷ 180 × 100 = 80% response rate**

Response rate should be considered when interpreting survey findings.

---

# 12. Favorability

A pulse survey may use favorable responses as another reporting measure.

**Favorable Response = Agree + Strongly Agree**

Example:

If 72 out of 100 respondents select either Agree or Strongly Agree:

**Favorable Score = 72%**

---

# 13. Trend Analysis

Where pulse surveys are repeated, results can be compared over time.

Example:

**Pulse 1 → Pulse 2 → Pulse 3 → Pulse 4**

Trend analysis can help identify:

* Improving areas
* Declining areas
* Stable areas
* Emerging concerns

Trends should be interpreted alongside organizational events and other relevant people data.

# 14. Action Planning

Pulse survey findings should result in practical actions where intervention is appropriate.

| Finding                      | Action                                  | Owner            | Timeline | Measure             |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------- | -------- | ------------------- |
| Communication score declined | Increase manager communication updates  | Managers         | 30 days  | Communication score |
| Recognition score declined   | Introduce team recognition practice     | Managers         | 30 days  | Recognition score   |
| Career score remains low     | Conduct career conversations            | HRBP + Managers  | 90 days  | Career score        |
| Workload concerns increased  | Review workload and resource allocation | Business Leaders | 60 days  | Workload feedback   |

# 15. Manager-Level Follow-Up

Where appropriate and where group sizes protect confidentiality, managers may receive aggregated team-level results.

Managers should:

* Review findings with their teams
* Identify practical actions
* Agree priorities
* Track implementation
* Provide updates

Managers should not attempt to identify individual employees based on survey responses.

---

# 16. HRBP Responsibilities

The HRBP should:

* Define the business question
* Select appropriate survey questions
* Advise on survey methodology
* Protect employee confidentiality
* Analyze findings
* Identify key themes
* Facilitate management discussions
* Recommend interventions
* Track actions
* Evaluate outcomes

---

# 17. Data Protection

The organization should:

* Protect survey responses
* Restrict access to authorized personnel
* Use appropriate reporting thresholds
* Avoid identifying individual respondents
* Store survey data securely
* Use aggregated results for leadership reporting where appropriate
