# HRBP Workforce Gap Analysis

## Executive Summary

This Workforce Gap Analysis evaluates a fictional workforce of **700 employees** across 10 departments and compares current headcount with modeled workforce requirements.

The analysis is designed to demonstrate how an HR Business Partner can translate workforce data into business-focused people recommendations.

### Headline Findings

- **Current workforce:** 700 employees
- **Required workforce:** 743 employees
- **Net workforce gap:** 43 employees
- **Gross additional headcount requirement:** 46 employees across departments with positive gaps
- **Potential surplus:** 3 employees in Administration
- **Monthly payroll:** ₦353,385,000
- **Annualized payroll:** ₦4,240,620,000
- **Average monthly salary:** ₦504,835.71
- **High turnover-risk employees:** 150 (21.4%)
- **Employees in critical roles:** 291 (41.6%)
- **Employees assessed as having partial skills coverage:** 116 (16.6%)
- **Average tenure:** 6.35 years
- **Average performance rating:** 3.47 / 5

> **Portfolio note:** All workforce data is fictional and was created for HRBP portfolio and analytics demonstration purposes.

---

## 1. Business Question

The core HRBP question is:

> **Does the current workforce have sufficient capacity and capability to support the organization's modeled workforce requirements, and what people interventions should management prioritize?**

The analysis therefore considers:

1. Headcount capacity
2. Workforce gaps
3. Critical-role exposure
4. Turnover risk
5. Skills coverage
6. Performance
7. Workforce cost
8. Recruitment and internal-mobility priorities

## 5. Turnover Risk

The dataset identifies:

| Turnover Risk | Employees | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Low | 300 | 42.9% |
| Medium | 250 | 35.7% |
| High | 150 | 21.4% |
| **Total** | **700** | **100%** |

### HRBP Interpretation

**150 employees are classified as high turnover risk.**

This is material because recruitment requirements may increase if high-risk employees leave before identified workforce gaps are addressed.

The HRBP should therefore avoid treating recruitment as the only solution.

Recommended actions include:

- Review high-risk employee populations by department.
- Conduct targeted stay interviews.
- Review compensation and career-development factors.
- Assess manager and employee-experience issues.
- Identify critical high-risk employees.
- Develop targeted retention actions.
- Monitor turnover-risk movement monthly.

---

## 6. Critical Roles

**291 employees (41.6%)** are identified as occupying critical roles.

This creates a significant business-continuity consideration.

The HRBP should assess:

- Whether every critical role has a documented successor.
- Whether key processes are documented.
- Whether knowledge is concentrated in a small number of employees.
- Whether critical-role employees have high turnover risk.
- Whether cross-training exists.
- Whether external talent pipelines are available.

### Recommended Intervention

Create a **Critical Role & Succession Matrix** containing:

| Critical Role | Incumbent | Successor | Readiness | Risk | Development Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 10. Recommended Workforce Strategy

### Priority 1 — Address Critical Workforce Gaps

Focus workforce planning attention on:

1. IT
2. Sales
3. Legal & Compliance
4. Human Resources
5. Customer Service
6. Operations

Before external recruitment, evaluate internal mobility and redeployment opportunities.

---

### Priority 2 — Review Administration Capacity

Administration has a modeled surplus of three employees.

The HRBP should assess whether these employees can be:

- Redeployed
- Reskilled
- Cross-trained
- Moved into approved vacancies
- Developed for future roles

This should occur before treating every positive departmental gap as an external hiring requirement.

---

### Priority 3 — Build a Retention Programme

With 150 employees classified as high turnover risk, management should establish a targeted retention programme.

Actions:

- Stay interviews
- Manager effectiveness reviews
- Career-path discussions
- Compensation benchmarking
- Recognition initiatives
- Learning opportunities
- Critical-talent retention plans

---

### Priority 4 — Strengthen Succession Planning

With 291 employees in identified critical roles, the organization should establish succession coverage for critical positions.

Recommended target:

> **100% of business-critical positions should have an identified succession status.**

Readiness can be classified as:

- Ready Now
- Ready in 1–2 Years
- Ready in 3+ Years
- No Identified Successor

---

### Priority 5 — Close Skills Gaps

The 116 employees with partial skills assessments should be reviewed through a structured skills-gap exercise.

The HRBP should distinguish between:

- Trainable gaps
- Recruitable gaps
- Critical specialist gaps
- Future capability gaps

## 14. HRBP Conclusion

The analysis indicates that the organization has a **700-person current workforce against a modeled requirement of 743 employees**, creating a **net workforce gap of 43 employees**.

However, the appropriate HR response is not simply to recruit 43 people.

The HRBP should consider the interaction between:

**Headcount + Skills + Performance + Turnover Risk + Critical Roles + Workforce Cost**

The dataset demonstrates a practical HRBP approach in which workforce decisions are connected to business requirements, internal talent capability and organizational risk.

The recommended approach is therefore to:

1. Prioritize critical workforce gaps.
2. Evaluate internal mobility before external recruitment.
3. Address high turnover risk through targeted retention.
4. Build succession coverage for critical roles.
5. Close trainable skills gaps through development.
6. Monitor workforce cost and business impact.
7. Review workforce requirements continuously as business conditions change.
