# Workforce Planning Framework

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this framework is to provide HR Business Partners and organizational leaders with a structured approach to determining current and future workforce requirements.

Workforce planning connects organizational strategy with people requirements by identifying the workforce capacity and capabilities required to achieve business objectives.

---

## 2. Workforce Planning Principles

Effective workforce planning should be:

- **Strategic** — aligned with organizational objectives.
- **Data-driven** — supported by reliable workforce information.
- **Forward-looking** — considers future workforce requirements.
- **Business-focused** — connected to operational and financial priorities.
- **Flexible** — capable of adapting to changes in business conditions.
- **Sustainable** — considers both immediate and long-term workforce needs.

---

## 3. Workforce Planning Cycle

### Stage 1 — Business Strategy Review

The HRBP works with leadership to understand:

- Business objectives
- Growth plans
- Expansion
- New products or services
- Operational changes
- Technology adoption
- Organizational restructuring

### Stage 2 — Current Workforce Assessment

Assess:

- Headcount
- Job roles
- Departments
- Skills
- Experience
- Tenure
- Performance
- Critical positions
- Leadership pipeline
- Attrition patterns

### Stage 3 — Workforce Demand Forecast

Determine the workforce required to support future business activities.

Consider:

- Planned expansion
- Increased workload
- New locations
- New business units
- Productivity requirements
- Technology
- Expected employee turnover

### Stage 4 — Workforce Supply Analysis

Assess the organization's ability to meet future workforce requirements through:

- Existing employees
- Internal mobility
- Promotions
- Succession
- Recruitment
- Training
- Reskilling
- Outsourcing

### Stage 5 — Gap Analysis

Compare workforce demand with workforce supply.

**Workforce Gap = Required Workforce − Available Workforce**

Gaps may be identified in:

- Headcount
- Skills
- Leadership
- Experience
- Critical roles
- Organizational capability

### Stage 6 — Workforce Action Plan

Develop appropriate interventions.

Possible actions include:

| Workforce Issue | Potential Intervention |
|---|---|
| Headcount shortage | Recruitment |
| Skills shortage | Training / reskilling |
| Leadership gap | Succession planning |
| Excess capacity | Redeployment |
| Critical-role dependency | Succession / talent pipeline |
| High turnover | Retention intervention |
| Productivity concerns | Performance intervention |

### Stage 7 — Implementation

The HRBP works with relevant stakeholders to implement the agreed workforce actions.

Stakeholders may include:

- Executive Management
- Department Heads
- Finance
- HR
- Recruitment
- Learning & Development
- Operations

### Stage 8 — Measurement & Review

Workforce plans should be reviewed regularly.

Key indicators include:

- Headcount
- Vacancy rate
- Turnover
- Time-to-fill
- Time-to-hire
- Absenteeism
- Productivity
- Succession coverage
- Skills coverage
- Workforce cost

---

## 4. HRBP Responsibilities

The HR Business Partner acts as the connection between workforce strategy and business strategy.

Key responsibilities include:

1. Understanding business objectives.
2. Translating business requirements into workforce requirements.
3. Providing workforce data and insights.
4. Identifying talent and capability gaps.
5. Advising management on workforce options.
6. Supporting workforce-cost planning.
7. Coordinating recruitment and development interventions.
8. Monitoring workforce metrics.
9. Reviewing workforce risks.
10. Reporting workforce insights to leadership.

---

## 5. Workforce Risk Assessment

Workforce risks should be identified and monitored.

Examples include:

### Critical Role Risk

A business may depend heavily on one individual for a critical function.

**Response:**
- Identify successors.
- Cross-train employees.
- Document critical processes.

### Skills Risk

The organization may lack employees with capabilities required for future operations.

**Response:**
- Upskill existing employees.
- Recruit specialized talent.
- Develop internal talent pipelines.

### Attrition Risk

High employee turnover may affect operational continuity.

**Response:**
- Analyze exit data.
- Identify turnover drivers.
- Review employee experience.
- Develop targeted retention interventions.

---

## 6. Workforce Planning Questions for Leadership

An HRBP should be able to ask:

### Business

- What are the organization's priorities for the next 12–36 months?
- Which business areas are expected to grow?
- Which areas may decline?
- What new capabilities will be required?

### Workforce

- Do we have enough employees?
- Do we have the right skills?
- Where are the biggest workforce gaps?
- Which positions are business-critical?

### Talent

- Who are our high-potential employees?
- Which positions have no identified successor?
- Where are our leadership gaps?
- What capabilities can be developed internally?

### Financial

- What is the projected workforce cost?
- Can the organization afford the required headcount?
- Should a capability be built internally or acquired externally?

---

## 7. Workforce Planning Output

A completed workforce planning exercise should produce:

- Current workforce profile
- Future workforce requirements
- Workforce gap analysis
- Skills gap analysis
- Critical-role analysis
- Succession requirements
- Recruitment requirements
- Development requirements
- Workforce cost projections
- HR action plan
- Workforce KPIs

---

## 8. Review Frequency

Workforce planning should be reviewed according to the organization's needs.

Recommended review points include:

- Annual strategic workforce planning
- Quarterly workforce reviews
- Monthly headcount monitoring
- Ad-hoc reviews following significant organizational changes

---

## 9. Conclusion

Effective workforce planning enables HR to move beyond administrative activities and participate directly in organizational decision-making.

The HRBP's role is to ensure that workforce decisions are connected to the organization's strategic direction, operational requirements and long-term capability needs.

**People strategy should support business strategy.**
