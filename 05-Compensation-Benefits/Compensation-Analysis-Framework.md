# Compensation Analysis Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework provides a structured approach for conducting compensation analysis. It helps the organization assess pay levels, identify inequities, monitor market competitiveness, and support evidence-based decisions on salary adjustments, offers, and structure changes.

The HR Business Partner (HRBP) uses compensation analysis to advise managers, support calibration, flag risks, and contribute to fair and defensible pay decisions.

---

## 2. Objectives

- Evaluate internal equity across roles, levels, and employee groups
- Assess external competitiveness against relevant markets
- Identify pay compression, inversion, or unexplained gaps
- Support annual salary review and off-cycle decisions
- Inform salary structure and midpoint adjustments
- Provide data for workforce budgeting and cost planning
- Strengthen the integrity and consistency of compensation practices

---

## 3. Types of Compensation Analysis

| Analysis Type              | Focus                                      | Typical Use Case                          |
|---------------------------|--------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| Internal Equity           | Pay differences for similar roles/levels   | Fairness reviews, calibration             |
| Market Competitiveness    | Positioning vs. external benchmarks        | Structure review, offer decisions         |
| Compa-Ratio Analysis      | Actual pay vs. range midpoint              | Salary review prioritization              |
| Compression / Inversion   | Pay relationships between levels or tenure | Promotion and retention risk              |
| Demographic Equity        | Pay patterns by gender, age, etc.          | Pay equity audits                         |
| Cost & Budget Analysis    | Payroll cost trends and projections        | Budgeting and scenario planning           |
| Offer & Acceptance        | Competitiveness of new hire packages       | Recruitment effectiveness                 |

---

## 4. Guiding Principles

- Use clean, accurate, and consistent data
- Compare like-for-like roles and levels
- Combine quantitative data with qualitative context
- Protect individual confidentiality at all times
- Document assumptions, filters, and limitations
- Focus on actionable insights, not just numbers
- Align analysis with compensation strategy and policy
