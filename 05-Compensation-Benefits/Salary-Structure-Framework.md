# Salary Structure Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework outlines how the organization designs, maintains, and applies its salary structure. A clear salary structure supports internal equity, external competitiveness, career progression, and consistent decision-making across the business.

The HR Business Partner (HRBP) uses this framework to guide managers, support salary decisions, and ensure pay practices remain fair, transparent (within limits), and aligned with the overall compensation strategy.

---

## 2. Objectives of the Salary Structure

- Provide clear pay ranges for roles at each level
- Support internal equity across similar jobs
- Enable external market competitiveness
- Facilitate career progression and promotions
- Guide hiring, promotion, and annual review decisions
- Control payroll costs within approved budgets
- Reduce pay compression and inconsistency

---

## 3. Core Elements of the Structure

### 3.1 Job Levels / Grades
Roles are grouped into levels or grades based on:
- Job evaluation results (or leveling methodology)
- Scope of responsibility
- Required knowledge, skills, and experience
- Decision-making authority
- Impact on the organization

### 3.2 Salary Bands / Ranges
Each level has a salary range that typically includes:
- **Minimum** – Entry point for the level
- **Midpoint** – Competitive target (often aligned to market median)
- **Maximum** – Upper limit for the level

Range spread (distance from minimum to maximum) usually increases at higher levels.

### 3.3 Midpoint Management
- Midpoints are regularly reviewed against market data
- Compa-ratio (actual salary ÷ midpoint) is a key monitoring metric
- Target positioning is generally around the midpoint for fully competent performers

---

## 4. Design Principles

| Principle              | Description                                                                 |
|------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Internal Equity        | Similar roles and contribution levels are treated consistently              |
| External Competitiveness | Ranges reflect relevant market data                                        |
| Flexibility            | Enough room within bands for performance and experience differentiation     |
| Progression Support    | Clear path for movement within and between levels                           |
| Simplicity             | Structure is understandable for managers and sustainable to maintain        |
| Affordability          | Aligned with organizational budget and financial capacity                   |

---

## 5. How the Structure is Used

### Hiring
- Offers are normally made within the approved range for the level
- Positioning within the range considers experience, skills, and internal peers
- Offers above midpoint or near maximum require additional justification and approval

### Promotions
- Movement to a higher level usually includes a salary adjustment into the new range
- Typical promotion increases are guided by policy (e.g., 8–15%, adjusted for position in new range)

### Annual Salary Review
- Increases consider performance, position in range (compa-ratio), budget, and market movement
- Employees below midpoint with strong performance are often prioritized for larger adjustments
- Employees near or above maximum may receive smaller increases or be considered for other rewards

### Lateral Moves
- Salary usually remains unchanged unless there is a significant change in scope or market value

---

## 6. Governance & Maintenance

- **Ownership**: Compensation / HR team designs and maintains the structure
- **Review Frequency**: Full structure review at least annually or when significant market shifts occur
- **Market Data**: Reliable salary surveys and benchmarking sources are used
- **Local Adjustments**: Country or location differentials may apply where relevant
- **Approval**: Changes to structure or midpoints require appropriate leadership approval

### HRBP Role
- Explain the structure to managers
- Support consistent application during hiring and reviews
- Highlight compression or equity issues
- Provide input on local market realities
- Ensure decisions stay within policy and authority matrix

---

## 7. Key Metrics

- Distribution of employees across the range (below / at / above midpoint)
- Average compa-ratio by level, function, and demographics
- Percentage of employees outside their range
- Promotion increase averages
- Time spent in level before promotion
- Pay compression indicators

---

## 8. Exception Handling

Exceptions to the salary structure (offers or adjustments outside range) should:
- Be documented with clear business rationale
- Include market data and internal equity analysis
- Follow the defined approval matrix
- Be tracked for pattern monitoring

---

## 9. Confidentiality & Documentation

- Individual salaries remain confidential
- Only anonymized or illustrative examples should appear in this portfolio
- Real employee data must never be uploaded
