# Annual Salary Review Process

## 1. Purpose

This document outlines the end-to-end process for conducting the organization’s annual salary review. The process ensures that pay decisions are fair, consistent, market-informed, performance-aligned, and within approved budgets.

The HR Business Partner (HRBP) plays a central role in guiding managers, facilitating calibration, monitoring equity, and supporting clear communication of outcomes.

---

## 2. Objectives

- Reward performance and contribution appropriately
- Maintain internal equity and reduce unjustified pay gaps
- Keep pace with relevant market movements
- Manage payroll costs within the approved budget
- Support retention of key talent
- Ensure consistent application of compensation principles
- Provide managers with clear guidelines and support

---

## 3. Guiding Principles

- Decisions are based on performance, position in range, market data, and budget
- Consistency and fairness across teams and functions
- Transparency of process (while protecting individual salary confidentiality)
- Affordability and alignment with business results
- Compliance with applicable laws and internal policies
- Documentation of key decisions and exceptions

---

## 4. High-Level Timeline (Typical Cycle)

| Phase                        | Timing              | Main Activities                                      |
|-----------------------------|---------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| Preparation & Planning      | 8–10 weeks before   | Budget setting, market data refresh, guidelines      |
| Manager Recommendations     | 4–6 weeks before    | Managers propose increases                           |
| Calibration & Review        | 2–4 weeks before    | HRBP-facilitated sessions, equity checks             |
| Final Approvals             | 1–2 weeks before    | Leadership sign-off                                  |
| Communication               | Implementation week | Manager conversations with employees                 |
| Payroll Implementation      | Agreed effective date | Updates processed                                  |
| Post-Cycle Review           | 2–4 weeks after     | Analysis of outcomes and lessons learned             |

## 5. Detailed Process Steps

### Step 1: Preparation
- Confirm overall budget envelope with Finance and Leadership
- Refresh market benchmarks and salary structure midpoints if needed
- Finalize performance ratings
- Issue clear increase guidelines (e.g., average %, ranges by performance and compa-ratio)
- Prepare data packs for managers (current salary, range position, performance rating, tenure, etc.)
- Communicate timeline, roles, and expectations to all stakeholders

### Step 2: Manager Recommendations
- Line managers review their team data
- Propose individual salary increases based on guidelines
- Flag retention risks, promotion cases, or equity concerns
- Submit recommendations by the deadline

### Step 3: Calibration
- HRBP facilitates calibration sessions with managers and relevant leaders
- Review proposed increases for consistency across teams
- Check for performance–pay alignment
- Identify and address potential equity or compression issues
- Adjust recommendations where needed to stay within budget and policy
- Document rationale for significant deviations

### Step 4: Final Review & Approval
- Consolidated recommendations reviewed by HR and Leadership
- Final budget confirmation
- Approval according to the authority matrix
- Exceptions formally recorded

### Step 5: Communication
- Managers hold individual conversations with employees
- Focus on performance, contribution, and future expectations
- Explain the decision clearly and respectfully
- HRBP supports managers with talking points and difficult conversation guidance where required

### Step 6: Implementation
- Approved changes submitted to Payroll
- Effective date applied consistently
- Confirmation records retained

### Step 7: Post-Cycle Review
- Analyze overall outcomes (average increase, distribution, budget utilization)
- Review equity metrics and any emerging patterns
- Gather feedback from managers
- Document lessons learned for the next cycle

## 6. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role              | Key Responsibilities                                              |
|-------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Leadership        | Set budget and strategic priorities; final approval               |
| Compensation / HR | Design guidelines, provide market data, oversee process integrity |
| HRBP              | Guide managers, facilitate calibration, monitor equity, support communication |
| Line Managers     | Make informed recommendations; conduct employee conversations     |
| Finance           | Confirm budget availability and cost impact                       |
| Payroll           | Accurate and timely implementation of approved changes            |

---

## 7. Decision Factors for Individual Increases

Typical factors considered (weighted according to company policy):
- Performance rating / contribution
- Current position in salary range (compa-ratio)
- Market competitiveness of the role
- Internal equity relative to peers
- Budget availability
- Retention risk / critical skills
- Time since last increase
- Promotion or significant role change

---

## 8. Key Metrics to Track

- Average increase percentage vs. budget
- Distribution of increases by performance rating
- Movement in compa-ratio distribution
- Percentage of employees receiving an increase
- Budget utilization rate
- Equity indicators (e.g., by gender, level, function)
- Number and nature of exceptions
- Manager and employee feedback on process quality

---

## 9. Exception Management

Any increase outside normal guidelines should:
- Have a clear written business rationale
- Include supporting data (market, equity, retention)
- Follow the defined approval path
- Be logged for monitoring and future reference

---

## 10. Confidentiality

- Individual salary information is strictly confidential
- Only aggregated or anonymized data may be used in reports or this portfolio
- Real employee data must never be uploaded to this repository
