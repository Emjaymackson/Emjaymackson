# 9-Box Talent Matrix

## Overview

The 9-Box Talent Matrix is a talent-management framework used to assess employees across two dimensions:

1. **Current Performance**
2. **Future Potential**

The framework helps HR Business Partners and management identify appropriate talent-development, succession and retention actions.

---

# 1. The 9-Box Framework

| | Low Potential | Medium Potential | High Potential |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High Performance** | Reliable Performer | High Performer | High Potential / Star |
| **Medium Performance** | Solid Contributor | Core Talent | Emerging Talent |
| **Low Performance** | Underperformer | Development Required | Potential / Development Required |

---

# 2. Box 1 — Low Performance / Low Potential

### Typical Profile

Employees currently demonstrating below-expected performance with limited evidence of readiness for significantly broader responsibilities.

### HRBP Considerations

- Performance diagnosis
- Role clarity
- Coaching
- Performance improvement plan where appropriate
- Skills assessment
- Manager support

### Recommended Action

**Manage Performance**

The HRBP should investigate the underlying causes before determining the appropriate intervention.

---

# 3. Box 2 — Low Performance / Medium Potential

### Typical Profile

Employees who may demonstrate future capability but are not currently meeting expected performance standards.

### HRBP Considerations

- Skills gaps
- Training
- Coaching
- Manager support
- Role fit
- Performance expectations

### Recommended Action

**Develop and Monitor**

---

# 4. Box 3 — Low Performance / High Potential

### Typical Profile

Employees who demonstrate indicators of future capability but whose current performance is below expectations.

### HRBP Considerations

The HRBP should investigate whether the issue relates to:

- Role fit
- Experience
- Training
- Managerial support
- Work environment
- Job expectations

### Recommended Action

**Diagnose → Develop → Monitor**

High potential should not automatically override current performance concerns.

---

# 5. Box 4 — Medium Performance / Low Potential

### Typical Profile

Employees who provide useful contribution within their current role but may have limited evidence of readiness for significantly broader responsibilities.

### HRBP Considerations

- Role stability
- Recognition
- Skill maintenance
- Engagement
- Career expectations

### Recommended Action

**Retain and Recognize**

---

# 6. Box 5 — Medium Performance / Medium Potential

### Typical Profile

Employees demonstrating consistent contribution with potential for further development.

### HRBP Considerations

- Development opportunities
- Coaching
- Stretch assignments
- Career conversations
- Skills development

### Recommended Action

**Develop**

---

# 7. Box 6 — Medium Performance / High Potential

### Typical Profile

Employees showing strong indicators of future capability but who may require additional experience or development before moving into larger responsibilities.

### HRBP Considerations

- Leadership development
- Stretch assignments
- Mentoring
- Cross-functional exposure
- Succession planning

### Recommended Action

**Accelerate Development**

---

# 8. Box 7 — High Performance / Low Potential

### Typical Profile

Employees delivering strong results within their current role without sufficient evidence that they are seeking or ready for significantly broader responsibilities.

### HRBP Considerations

- Recognition
- Retention
- Specialist career path
- Expert-role development
- Knowledge transfer

### Recommended Action

**Retain and Leverage Expertise**

---

# 9. Box 8 — High Performance / Medium Potential

### Typical Profile

Employees consistently delivering strong results and demonstrating potential for broader responsibilities.

### HRBP Considerations

- Stretch assignments
- Leadership development
- Mentoring
- Succession planning
- Internal mobility

### Recommended Action

**Develop for Broader Responsibility**

---

# 10. Box 9 — High Performance / High Potential

### Typical Profile

Employees demonstrating strong current performance and indicators of readiness for significantly broader responsibilities.

### HRBP Considerations

- Succession planning
- Leadership development
- Strategic projects
- Executive mentoring
- Retention planning
- Accelerated career development

### Recommended Action

**Develop, Retain and Prepare for Critical Roles**

---

# 11. Talent Review Process

The HRBP should not place employees into the 9-Box based solely on personal opinion.

The recommended process is:

### Step 1 — Collect Evidence

Review:

- Performance ratings
- Objectives achieved
- Skills
- Competencies
- Career aspirations
- Leadership behaviours
- Learning agility
- Critical-role requirements

### Step 2 — Initial Assessment

The employee's manager provides an initial assessment.

### Step 3 — HRBP Review

The HRBP reviews the assessment for:

- Consistency
- Evidence
- Bias
- Calibration
- Organizational context

### Step 4 — Talent Calibration

Leadership and HR discuss differences in assessments.

### Step 5 — Final Placement

The employee is assigned to the most appropriate 9-Box category based on available evidence.

### Step 6 — Development Action

Every employee should have an appropriate talent action.

# 12. Talent Actions by Box

| Talent Category | Primary HR Action |
|---|---|
| Low Performance / Low Potential | Performance management |
| Low Performance / Medium Potential | Development and monitoring |
| Low Performance / High Potential | Diagnose and develop |
| Medium Performance / Low Potential | Retain and recognize |
| Medium Performance / Medium Potential | Develop |
| Medium Performance / High Potential | Accelerate development |
| High Performance / Low Potential | Retain expertise |
| High Performance / Medium Potential | Broaden responsibility |
| High Performance / High Potential | Succession and leadership pipeline |

---

# 13. 9-Box Assessment Criteria

## Performance

Performance may consider:

- Achievement of objectives
- Quality of work
- Productivity
- Customer outcomes
- Behavioural competencies
- Manager assessment

Performance should be based on evidence rather than personality or reputation.

---

## Potential

Potential may consider:

- Learning agility
- Ability to handle increased complexity
- Leadership capability
- Adaptability
- Strategic thinking
- Collaboration
- Capacity for broader responsibility

Potential should not be confused with popularity or personal relationships.

---

# 14. HRBP Governance

The 9-Box process should be governed carefully.

The HRBP should monitor for:

- Manager bias
- Recency bias
- Affinity bias
- Halo effect
- Stereotyping
- Inconsistent standards between departments
- Unsupported potential assessments

Talent placement should be evidence-based and periodically reviewed.

---

# 15. Link to Succession Planning

The 9-Box should feed directly into succession planning.

Employees identified as:

- High Performance / High Potential
- High Performance / Medium Potential
- Medium Performance / High Potential

may form part of the organization's future talent pipeline, subject to role-specific capability requirements.

However, 9-Box placement alone does not establish that an employee is ready to succeed a particular role.

---

# 16. HRBP Decision Framework

The HRBP should connect:

**Performance + Potential + Skills + Career Aspirations + Business Need**

before recommending a talent action.

For example:

### High Performance + High Potential + Critical Skill

Potential succession candidate.

### High Performance + Low Potential + Critical Expertise

Potential specialist or knowledge-retention priority.

### Low Performance + High Potential

Requires diagnosis and development before succession consideration.

---

# 17. Talent Review Output

A completed talent review should provide management with:

- Talent distribution
- High-potential population
- High-performance population
- Critical-role successors
- Development priorities
- Retention risks
- Succession gaps
- Recommended HR interventions

---

# Conclusion

The 9-Box Talent Matrix is a decision-support framework rather than a permanent label.

Employees can move between categories as:

- Performance changes
- Skills develop
- Roles change
- Business requirements change
- Leadership capability develops

The HRBP's responsibility is to ensure that talent decisions remain evidence-based, consistent and connected to business strategy.
