# Individual Development Plan (IDP)

## Overview

An Individual Development Plan (IDP) is a structured development framework used to help employees build the skills, experience and capabilities required for their current role and future career objectives.

The HRBP uses the IDP to connect:

**Employee Capability + Career Aspiration + Business Need + Development Action**

---

# 1. Purpose

The IDP is designed to:

- Identify employee development needs
- Close capability gaps
- Support career development
- Improve performance
- Prepare employees for future roles
- Support succession planning
- Improve internal mobility
- Strengthen employee engagement
- Build organizational capability

---

# 2. IDP Framework

The process follows:

**Assess → Identify → Plan → Develop → Measure → Review**

---

# 3. Step 1 — Assess Current Capability

The employee's current capability should be assessed using evidence such as:

- Performance results
- Skills assessment
- Competency assessment
- Manager feedback
- Employee self-assessment
- Previous development activities
- Role requirements
- Career aspirations

---

# 4. Step 2 — Identify Development Gaps

A development gap exists when there is a difference between:

**Current Capability**

and

**Required Capability**

for the employee's current or future role.

### Example

Current Role:

**HR Officer**

Future Role:

**HR Business Partner**

Current Capability:

- Recruitment
- Employee records
- HR administration
- Basic employee relations

Required Capability:

- Workforce planning
- HR analytics
- Strategic HR
- Business partnering
- Management consulting
- Workforce forecasting

Development gaps may therefore include:

**Strategic HR + Analytics + Business Partnering**

---

# 5. Development Categories

Development needs can be classified into:

### Technical Skills

Job-specific knowledge and technical capability.

### Behavioural Competencies

Examples:

- Communication
- Leadership
- Collaboration
- Problem solving
- Adaptability
- Accountability

### Leadership Capability

Examples:

- People management
- Delegation
- Decision-making
- Strategic thinking
- Conflict management

### Business Capability

Examples:

- Commercial awareness
- Financial understanding
- Customer understanding
- Business strategy
- Operational knowledge

---

# 6. Development Methods

Development should not rely only on classroom training.

Possible interventions include:

## Training

Formal learning programmes designed to build specific knowledge or skills.

---

## Coaching

Structured guidance focused on improving performance or capability.

---

## Mentoring

Longer-term development support from an experienced professional.

---

## Stretch Assignment

An assignment that exposes the employee to responsibilities beyond their current role.

---

## Job Rotation

Temporary movement across functions to broaden organizational understanding.

---

## Acting Assignment

Temporary responsibility for a higher or broader-level role.

---

## Project Leadership

Leading a project to develop decision-making, communication and leadership capabilities.

---

## Job Shadowing

Observing an experienced employee to gain practical knowledge.

---

## Self-Directed Learning

Independent learning through:

- Books
- Courses
- Professional certifications
- Research
- Industry resources

# 8. SMART Development Objectives

Development objectives should be:

**Specific**

Clearly define what the employee needs to achieve.

**Measurable**

Define how progress will be measured.

**Achievable**

Ensure the objective is realistic.

**Relevant**

Connect the development objective to the employee's role or career path.

**Time-Bound**

Define a clear completion period.

---

# 9. Example IDP

## Employee

Employee A

## Current Role

Operations Supervisor

## Target Role

Operations Manager

## Development Gap

Strategic planning and financial management.

## Development Objective

Develop the ability to prepare and manage operational plans and departmental budgets.

## Development Actions

1. Complete an operations management programme.
2. Shadow the Operations Manager.
3. Participate in annual budgeting.
4. Lead one cross-functional operational project.
5. Present monthly operational performance reports.

## Timeline

12 months.

## Success Measures

- Successfully prepares departmental operating plan.
- Demonstrates understanding of departmental budget.
- Leads cross-functional project successfully.
- Receives satisfactory manager assessment.
- Demonstrates improved strategic decision-making.

# 11. 70-20-10 Development Model

A practical development model can use:

### 70% — Experience

Learning through:

- Projects
- Stretch assignments
- Job rotation
- Acting responsibilities
- Problem solving

### 20% — Exposure

Learning through:

- Coaching
- Mentoring
- Feedback
- Networking
- Leadership exposure

### 10% — Formal Learning

Learning through:

- Training
- Courses
- Certifications
- Workshops
- Academic programmes

The percentages are a development framework rather than a mandatory formula.

---

# 12. IDP and Succession Planning

IDPs should connect directly to succession planning.

For example:

**Critical Role:**

Head of Operations

**Potential Successor:**

Operations Supervisor

**Readiness:**

1–2 Years

**Development Gaps:**

- Strategic planning
- Financial management
- Executive communication

**Development Actions:**

- Strategic project
- Budget participation
- Executive presentations
- Leadership coaching

The IDP therefore becomes the mechanism through which succession readiness is developed.

---

# 13. IDP and the 9-Box

The 9-Box Talent Matrix can help determine the appropriate development intensity.

### High Performance / High Potential

Possible focus:

- Leadership development
- Succession preparation
- Strategic exposure
- Stretch assignments

### High Performance / Medium Potential

Possible focus:

- Broader responsibility
- Specialist development
- Cross-functional exposure

### Medium Performance / High Potential

Possible focus:

- Coaching
- Skills development
- Role experience
- Performance support

### Low Performance / High Potential

Possible focus:

- Root-cause diagnosis
- Coaching
- Role-fit assessment
- Structured performance support

The development plan should be based on individual evidence rather than the 9-Box category alone.

---

# 14. Career Development

The HRBP should encourage structured career conversations covering:

- Current strengths
- Development areas
- Career aspirations
- Target roles
- Required skills
- Mobility preferences
- Development opportunities
- Timeline

Career aspirations should be considered alongside business requirements and available opportunities.

---

# 15. Manager Responsibilities

Managers are responsible for:

- Providing feedback
- Supporting development
- Creating opportunities
- Monitoring progress
- Removing development barriers
- Reviewing outcomes

The manager should not delegate the entire development process to HR.

---

# 16. Employee Responsibilities

Employees are responsible for:

- Participating actively
- Completing agreed development activities
- Seeking feedback
- Applying new skills
- Tracking progress
- Communicating development needs

---

# 17. HRBP Responsibilities

The HRBP should:

- Facilitate the development framework
- Identify organizational capability needs
- Support managers
- Monitor development trends
- Connect IDPs to succession planning
- Identify learning priorities
- Track development outcomes
- Support internal mobility

---

# 18. Development Review Cycle

Recommended review cycle:

### Initial Assessment

Identify current capability and development needs.

### 90-Day Review

Assess early progress.

### 6-Month Review

Evaluate development outcomes and adjust the plan.

### 12-Month Review

Assess overall development and future readiness.

The review frequency can be adjusted according to business and employee needs.

---

# 19. Development KPIs

The HRBP can track:

- IDP completion rate
- Development-plan participation
- Training completion
- Skills-gap closure
- Internal promotion rate
- Internal mobility rate
- Succession readiness
- Manager satisfaction
- Employee development satisfaction

---

# 20. IDP Completion Rate

### Formula

Completed Development Plans ÷ Required Development Plans × 100

---

# 21. Skills Gap Closure Rate

### Formula

Skills Gaps Closed ÷ Total Identified Skills Gaps × 100

This measures whether development activities are actually improving organizational capability.

---

# 22. Development Effectiveness

Completion of a training course does not automatically demonstrate capability improvement.

The HRBP should evaluate:

**Learning → Application → Behaviour → Performance → Business Outcome**

For example:

Training completed:

**Strategic Planning**

Application:

Employee prepares departmental strategic plan.

Behaviour:

Employee demonstrates stronger strategic decision-making.

Performance:

Operational targets improve.

Business Outcome:

Improved operational efficiency.

---

# 23. HRBP Development Review Questions

1. What capability does the employee currently have?
2. What capability is required?
3. What is the development gap?
4. What development action will close the gap?
5. Who owns the development action?
6. When should it be completed?
7. How will success be measured?
8. Has the employee applied the new capability?
9. Has performance improved?
10. Does the employee's development support a future organizational need?

---

# 24. Confidentiality

Individual development plans contain employee information and should be handled appropriately.

Access should be restricted to authorized individuals such as:

- Employee
- Line Manager
- HR / HRBP
- Relevant leadership where appropriate

Development information should be used responsibly and consistently with applicable organizational policies and data-protection requirements.

---

# Conclusion

An effective IDP converts talent-management findings into practical development action.

The HRBP should connect:

**Performance → Potential → Capability Gap → Development → Readiness → Business Need**

The objective is not simply to train employees.

The objective is to build measurable capability that improves both employee career development and organizational performance.
