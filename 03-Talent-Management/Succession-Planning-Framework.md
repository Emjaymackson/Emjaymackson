# Succession Planning Framework

## Overview

Succession planning is a structured HR process for identifying and developing employees who may be capable of assuming critical roles in the future.

The purpose is to reduce business-continuity risk and ensure that the organization develops the capabilities required for future leadership and critical positions.

---

# 1. Succession Planning Objectives

The framework is designed to:

- Identify critical organizational roles
- Assess succession risk
- Identify potential successors
- Measure successor readiness
- Identify capability gaps
- Create development plans
- Reduce key-person dependency
- Strengthen internal talent pipelines
- Support business continuity
- Improve leadership readiness

---

# 2. Succession Planning Framework

The process follows:

**Identify → Assess → Select → Develop → Monitor → Review**

---

# 3. Step 1 — Identify Critical Roles

A critical role is a position where vacancy or loss of capability could significantly affect:

- Business continuity
- Revenue
- Operations
- Customer relationships
- Regulatory compliance
- Strategic execution
- Specialized organizational knowledge
- Leadership continuity

Examples may include:

- Chief Executive Officer
- Chief Financial Officer
- Head of Human Resources
- Head of Technology
- Head of Sales
- Operations Director
- Key technical specialists

Criticality should be determined by business impact rather than job title alone.

---

# 4. Critical Role Assessment

Each role should be assessed using factors such as:

| Factor | Assessment Question |
|---|---|
| Business Impact | How significantly would the business be affected if the role became vacant? |
| Vacancy Risk | How difficult would the role be to replace? |
| Skill Scarcity | Are the required capabilities difficult to find? |
| Knowledge Dependency | Does the role hold significant institutional knowledge? |
| Revenue Impact | Does the role directly influence revenue? |
| Operational Impact | Would operations be disrupted? |
| Compliance Impact | Could vacancy create regulatory or legal risk? |
| Leadership Impact | Does the role influence organizational direction? |

---

# 5. Succession Risk

Succession risk should consider both:

**Role Criticality + Successor Readiness**

A highly critical role with no suitable successor represents a significant continuity risk.

---

# 6. Step 2 — Identify Potential Successors

Potential successors may be identified from:

- Current employees
- Internal talent pools
- High-potential employees
- High-performing specialists
- Emerging leaders
- Internal mobility candidates

External candidates may also be considered where internal capability is unavailable.

---

# 7. Step 3 — Assess Successor Readiness

Successors should be categorized according to readiness.

## Ready Now

The employee has the capability and experience required to assume the role with limited additional preparation.

---

## Ready in 1–2 Years

The employee has strong potential but requires additional development, experience or exposure.

---

## Ready in 3+ Years

The employee may have long-term potential but requires significant development before assuming the role.

---

## No Identified Successor

There is currently no identified internal employee with sufficient evidence of readiness or potential.

This category should trigger a succession-risk review.

---

# 8. Succession Readiness Assessment

Potential successors should be assessed against the requirements of the target role.

Assessment areas may include:

- Technical capability
- Leadership capability
- Strategic thinking
- Commercial awareness
- Decision-making
- Communication
- Stakeholder management
- Industry knowledge
- People management
- Problem solving
- Relevant experience
- Behavioural competencies

---

# 9. Successor Readiness Matrix

| Readiness | Meaning | Typical HRBP Action |
|---|---|---|
| Ready Now | Can assume role with limited additional preparation | Maintain readiness and retention plan |
| Ready in 1–2 Years | Requires targeted development | Accelerated development plan |
| Ready in 3+ Years | Long-term potential | Structured development pipeline |
| No Successor | No suitable internal successor identified | External pipeline + internal capability building |

---

# 10. Step 4 — Identify Development Gaps

For every potential successor, compare:

**Current Capability vs Future Role Requirement**

Typical gaps may include:

- Leadership
- Financial management
- Strategic planning
- Technical knowledge
- People management
- Commercial experience
- Regulatory knowledge
- Stakeholder management
- Cross-functional exposure

---

# 11. Development Interventions

Development should be matched to the identified gap.

Possible interventions include:

### Coaching

One-to-one support focused on specific capability or performance areas.

### Mentoring

Structured guidance from an experienced leader.

### Stretch Assignment

Temporary responsibility for projects or activities outside the employee's normal scope.

### Job Rotation

Exposure to different functions or business areas.

### Acting Assignment

Temporary assumption of responsibilities associated with the target role.

### Training

Formal development focused on specific knowledge or skills.

### Cross-Functional Exposure

Experience working with other departments.

### Executive Exposure

Participation in strategic meetings and leadership initiatives.

---

# 12. Individual Succession Development Plan

Each identified successor should have a documented development plan.

The plan should include:

| Area | Example |
|---|---|
| Target Role | Head of Operations |
| Current Role | Operations Manager |
| Readiness | Ready in 1–2 Years |
| Capability Gap | Strategic planning |
| Development Action | Strategic project leadership |
| Owner | Line Manager / HRBP |
| Timeline | 12 months |
| Success Measure | Successfully leads strategic project |

---

# 13. Succession Planning Dashboard

A management dashboard should monitor:

- Number of critical roles
- Critical roles with successors
- Critical roles without successors
- Ready-now successors
- 1–2 year successors
- 3+ year successors
- Critical capability gaps
- Development-plan completion
- Succession risk

---

# 14. Succession Coverage

## Formula

Critical Roles With At Least One Identified Successor ÷ Total Critical Roles × 100

### Example

If an organization has:

- 20 critical roles
- 15 with identified successors

Succession Coverage:

15 ÷ 20 × 100 = **75%**

---

# 15. Ready-Now Coverage

## Formula

Critical Roles With Ready-Now Successor ÷ Total Critical Roles × 100

This measures how much of the critical-role population has immediate internal succession capability.

---

# 16. Succession Risk Categories

### Low Risk

A critical role has one or more capable successors with appropriate readiness.

### Moderate Risk

A successor exists but requires significant development.

### High Risk

No ready successor exists and the role has substantial business impact.

---

# 17. HRBP Intervention Model

Where succession risk is identified, the HRBP should consider:

### Immediate

- Emergency continuity plan
- Knowledge transfer
- Documentation
- Interim leadership arrangements

### Short Term

- Identify internal successor
- Create development plan
- Begin mentoring/coaching
- Increase cross-functional exposure

### Medium Term

- Build talent pipeline
- Develop leadership capability
- Review internal mobility
- Strengthen recruitment pipeline

### Long Term

- Integrate succession planning into workforce planning
- Review organizational capability
- Build future leadership capacity

---

# 18. Succession Planning Governance

Succession planning should be reviewed periodically.

Recommended participants include:

- CEO / Managing Director
- Executive Leadership
- HR Business Partner
- Department Heads
- Relevant functional leaders

The HRBP should facilitate the process and maintain appropriate confidentiality.

---

# 19. Important HRBP Principle

Being identified as a potential successor does **not** guarantee promotion.

Succession planning is a risk-management and talent-development process.

Final appointments should consider:

- Business requirements
- Role requirements
- Employee performance
- Capability
- Experience
- Organizational circumstances
- Fair and consistent selection processes

---

# 20. Connection to Workforce Planning

Succession planning should connect directly to workforce planning.

### Workforce Planning asks:

**"What capabilities will the organization need?"**

### Succession Planning asks:

**"Who could provide those capabilities?"**

### Development Planning asks:

**"What must we do to prepare them?"**

Together:

**Workforce Planning → Talent Identification → Succession → Development → Business Continuity**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Head of Operations | Current Holder | Employee A | Ready Now | None | Maintain exposure | Low |
| Finance Director | Current Holder | Employee B | 1–2 Years | Strategic Finance | Mentoring | Moderate |

| Sales Director | Current Holder | None | No Successor | Multiple | External + internal pipeline | High |

---

# 22. HRBP Review Questions

During each succession review, the HRBP should ask:

1. Which roles are business-critical?
2. Which roles have no identified successor?
3. Which successors are ready now?
4. Which successors require development?
5. What capability gaps exist?
6. Are development actions being completed?
7. Are critical employees at risk of leaving?
8. Is institutional knowledge being transferred?
9. Are internal mobility opportunities being used?
10. Does the succession plan reflect future business strategy?

---

# Conclusion

Effective succession planning is not simply about naming replacements.

It is about systematically building organizational capability so that critical roles can remain resilient when people leave, change roles or become unavailable.

The HRBP should connect:

**Critical Roles + Talent + Readiness + Development + Risk**

to create a sustainable leadership and capability pipeline.
