# HR Metrics Dictionary

## Overview

This document defines key Human Resources metrics used in workforce analytics and HR Business Partnering.

The purpose is to establish consistent definitions, calculations and interpretations for people-related data.

---

# 1. Workforce Metrics

## 1.1 Headcount

### Definition

The number of employees working for an organization at a specific point in time.

### Formula

Headcount = Number of Active Employees

### HRBP Use

Used to understand workforce size and support:

- Workforce planning
- Budgeting
- Organizational design
- Workforce forecasting

---

## 1.2 Headcount by Department

### Definition

The number of employees assigned to each organizational department.

### HRBP Use

Helps identify:

- Workforce concentration
- Staffing requirements
- Departmental growth
- Workforce gaps

---

## 1.3 Workforce Gap

### Definition

The difference between required workforce capacity and current workforce capacity.

### Formula

Required Headcount − Current Headcount = Workforce Gap

### Interpretation

A positive result indicates additional workforce requirements.

A negative result may indicate potential excess capacity.

---

# 2. Recruitment Metrics

## 2.1 Time-to-Hire

### Definition

The number of days between a candidate entering the recruitment process and accepting an offer.

### HRBP Use

Helps evaluate recruitment efficiency.

---

## 2.2 Time-to-Fill

### Definition

The number of days between a position becoming vacant and the position being filled.

### HRBP Use

Useful for identifying recruitment bottlenecks and operational staffing risks.

---

## 2.3 Cost-per-Hire

### Definition

The average cost incurred to recruit one employee.

### Formula

Total Recruitment Cost ÷ Number of Hires

### HRBP Use

Supports recruitment-budget decisions.

---

## 2.4 Offer Acceptance Rate

### Formula

Accepted Offers ÷ Total Offers × 100

### HRBP Use

A declining rate may indicate issues involving:

- Compensation
- Employer brand
- Candidate experience
- Job expectations
- Market competitiveness

---

# 3. Retention Metrics

## 3.1 Employee Turnover Rate

### Formula

Number of Employee Exits ÷ Average Headcount × 100

### HRBP Use

Used to monitor workforce stability.

---

## 3.2 Voluntary Turnover

### Definition

The proportion of employees who leave the organization voluntarily.

Examples include:

- Resignation
- Career move
- Relocation
- Personal reasons

---

## 3.3 Retention Rate

### Formula

Employees Retained During Period ÷ Employees at Start of Period × 100

### HRBP Use

Measures the organization's ability to retain employees.

---

## 3.4 Employee Tenure

### Definition

The length of time an employee has worked for the organization.

### HRBP Use

Useful for analyzing:

- Workforce stability
- Experience distribution
- Succession planning
- Retention patterns

---

# 4. Performance Metrics

## 4.1 Average Performance Rating

### Formula

Total Performance Ratings ÷ Number of Employees Rated

### HRBP Use

Provides an overview of workforce performance.

It should not be interpreted without considering:

- Role differences
- Manager differences
- Performance-rating calibration
- Employee tenure
- Job complexity

---

## 4.2 Performance Distribution

Measures the percentage of employees falling within each performance category.

Example:

- Below Expectations
- Meets Expectations
- Exceeds Expectations
- Exceptional

### HRBP Use

Helps identify:

- High performers
- Development populations
- Performance-management requirements
- Talent pipelines

---

# 5. Skills Metrics

## 5.1 Skills Gap

### Definition

The difference between the capabilities required for a role and the capabilities currently available.

### HRBP Use

Supports:

- Training
- Reskilling
- Recruitment
- Succession planning
- Organizational capability development

---

## 5.2 Skills Coverage

### Definition

The extent to which the workforce possesses the skills required by the organization.

### HRBP Use

Helps determine whether the organization should:

- Build skills internally
- Buy skills externally
- Borrow capability
- Automate work

---

# 6. Critical Workforce Metrics

## 6.1 Critical Role

### Definition

A position that has significant importance to business continuity, revenue, compliance, operations or strategic capability.

### HRBP Use

Critical roles should receive appropriate:

- Succession planning
- Risk assessment
- Talent development
- Retention planning

---

## 6.2 Critical Role Coverage

### Definition

The percentage of critical positions with an identified successor.

### Formula

Critical Roles With Successors ÷ Total Critical Roles × 100

### HRBP Use

Measures succession readiness.

---

# 7. Absence Metrics

## 7.1 Absenteeism Rate

### Formula

Total Days Absent ÷ Total Available Working Days × 100

### HRBP Use

Helps identify:

- Workforce availability issues
- Employee wellbeing concerns
- Managerial issues
- Operational disruptions

---

# 8. Compensation Metrics

## 8.1 Average Salary

### Formula

Total Employee Salary ÷ Number of Employees

### HRBP Use

Used for:

- Workforce budgeting
- Salary analysis
- Compensation planning
- Workforce-cost forecasting

---

## 8.2 Payroll Cost

### Definition

The total compensation cost associated with employees over a defined period.

### HRBP Use

Supports workforce-cost planning and financial decision-making.

---

## 8.3 Salary Range

Measures the minimum and maximum compensation within a defined population.

### HRBP Use

Useful for identifying:

- Salary compression
- Pay inconsistencies
- Compensation structures
- Market positioning

---

# 9. Employee Engagement Metrics

## 9.1 Employee Engagement Score

A measurement of employee connection, commitment and willingness to contribute to organizational objectives.

### HRBP Use

Used to identify:

- Engagement strengths
- Engagement risks
- Managerial issues
- Employee-experience opportunities

---

## 9.2 Employee Net Promoter Score (eNPS)

### Formula

% Promoters − % Detractors

### HRBP Use

Provides an indicator of employee advocacy and experience.

---

# 10. Learning & Development Metrics

## 10.1 Training Participation Rate

### Formula

Employees Participating in Training ÷ Eligible Employees × 100

---

## 10.2 Training Completion Rate

### Formula

Employees Completing Training ÷ Employees Enrolled × 100

---

## 10.3 Training Effectiveness

Training effectiveness should be assessed through measures such as:

- Knowledge improvement
- Skill application
- Performance improvement
- Manager feedback
- Business outcomes

---

# 11. HRBP Decision Framework

HR metrics should not be viewed in isolation.

A strong HRBP analysis connects multiple indicators.

For example:

**High Turnover + Critical Role + Skills Gap**

may indicate a significant workforce continuity risk.

Similarly:

**Workforce Gap + High Recruitment Cost + Internal Skills Availability**

may justify evaluating internal mobility before external recruitment.

---

# 12. Data Interpretation Principles

HR data should be:

- Accurate
- Consistent
- Relevant
- Timely
- Contextualized
- Confidential
- Action-oriented

Numbers should be interpreted alongside business circumstances.

---

## Conclusion

HR analytics becomes valuable when data is translated into business insight and actionable people strategy.

The HRBP's role is to move from:

**Data → Insight → Recommendation → Action → Measurement**
