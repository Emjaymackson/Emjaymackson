# HR Metrics Dictionary

## Overview

This document defines key Human Resources metrics used in workforce analytics and HR Business Partnering.

The purpose is to establish consistent definitions, calculations and interpretation standards for people-related data.

---

# 1. Workforce Metrics

## 1.1 Headcount

### Definition

Headcount is the number of active employees in an organization at a specific point in time.

### Formula

Headcount = Number of Active Employees

### HRBP Use

Headcount is used for:

- Workforce planning
- Organizational design
- Budgeting
- Workforce forecasting
- Capacity planning

---

## 1.2 Headcount by Department

### Definition

The number of employees assigned to each organizational department.

### HRBP Use

This helps identify:

- Workforce concentration
- Staffing requirements
- Departmental growth
- Workforce gaps
- Organizational structure

---

## 1.3 Workforce Gap

### Definition

The difference between the workforce required by the business and the workforce currently available.

### Formula

Required Headcount − Current Headcount = Workforce Gap

### Interpretation

A positive result indicates that additional workforce capacity may be required.

A negative result may indicate potential excess capacity.

### HRBP Use

Used to support:

- Recruitment planning
- Internal mobility
- Redeployment
- Workforce restructuring
- Budget planning

---

## 1.4 Workforce Growth Rate

### Formula

(Current Headcount − Previous Headcount) ÷ Previous Headcount × 100

### HRBP Use

Measures how quickly the workforce is expanding or contracting.

---

# 2. Recruitment Metrics

## 2.1 Time-to-Hire

### Definition

The number of days between a candidate entering the recruitment process and accepting an employment offer.

### HRBP Use

Helps evaluate recruitment efficiency and identify delays in the hiring process.

---

## 2.2 Time-to-Fill

### Definition

The number of days between a position becoming vacant or being approved and the position being filled.

### HRBP Use

Useful for identifying:

- Recruitment bottlenecks
- Critical vacancy risks
- Hiring-process delays
- Operational staffing problems

---

## 2.3 Cost-per-Hire

### Definition

The average recruitment cost incurred for each employee hired.

### Formula

Total Recruitment Cost ÷ Number of Hires

### HRBP Use

Supports recruitment budgeting and recruitment-channel evaluation.

---

## 2.4 Offer Acceptance Rate

### Formula

Accepted Offers ÷ Total Offers × 100

### HRBP Use

A declining offer acceptance rate may warrant review of:

- Compensation
- Employer brand
- Candidate experience
- Job expectations
- Recruitment communication
- Market conditions

---

## 2.5 Recruitment Conversion Rate

### Definition

The percentage of candidates who progress from one stage of the recruitment funnel to another.

### Example

Candidates Interviewed ÷ Candidates Applied × 100

### HRBP Use

Helps identify recruitment funnel inefficiencies.

---

# 3. Retention Metrics

## 3.1 Employee Turnover Rate

### Definition

The percentage of employees who leave an organization during a specified period.

### Formula

Number of Employee Exits ÷ Average Headcount × 100

### HRBP Use

Used to monitor workforce stability and identify retention issues.

---

## 3.2 Voluntary Turnover Rate

### Definition

The percentage of employees who leave the organization voluntarily.

Examples include:

- Resignation
- Career move
- Relocation
- Personal reasons

### HRBP Use

Particularly useful for understanding controllable retention risks.

---

## 3.3 Involuntary Turnover Rate

### Definition

The percentage of employees whose employment ends through an employer-initiated decision.

Examples may include:

- Redundancy
- Dismissal
- End of contract
- Termination in accordance with applicable policy and law

---

## 3.4 Retention Rate

### Formula

Employees Retained During Period ÷ Employees at Start of Period × 100

### HRBP Use

Measures the organization's ability to retain employees over a defined period.

---

## 3.5 Employee Tenure

### Definition

The length of time an employee has worked for the organization.

### HRBP Use

Tenure analysis can support:

- Succession planning
- Retention analysis
- Workforce stability assessment
- Career development
- Experience planning

---

## 3.6 Early Attrition Rate

### Definition

The percentage of new employees who leave within a defined early-tenure period.

For example, an organization may monitor employees who leave within their first 90 or 180 days.

### HRBP Use

Can highlight issues involving:

- Recruitment quality
- Onboarding
- Job expectations
- Manager effectiveness
- Employee experience

---

# 4. Performance Metrics

## 4.1 Average Performance Rating

### Formula

Total Performance Ratings ÷ Number of Employees Rated

### HRBP Use

Provides an overall view of workforce performance.

Performance ratings should be interpreted alongside:

- Role differences
- Manager differences
- Rating calibration
- Employee tenure
- Job complexity

---

## 4.2 Performance Distribution

### Definition

The percentage of employees falling into each performance category.

Example:

- Below Expectations
- Meets Expectations
- Exceeds Expectations
- Exceptional

### HRBP Use

Helps identify:

- High performers
- Development populations
- Performance-management requirements
- Talent pipelines

---

## 4.3 High-Performer Population

### Definition

The number or percentage of employees who meet the organization's defined high-performance criteria.

### HRBP Use

High performers can be considered for:

- Succession planning
- Leadership development
- Stretch assignments
- Critical-role pipelines
- Recognition programmes

---

# 5. Skills & Capability Metrics

## 5.1 Skills Gap

### Definition

The difference between the capabilities required for a role and the capabilities currently available within the workforce.

### HRBP Use

Supports:

- Training
- Reskilling
- Recruitment
- Succession planning
- Organizational capability development

---

## 5.2 Skills Coverage

### Definition

The extent to which the workforce possesses the skills required to execute current and future business activities.

### HRBP Use

Helps determine whether the organization should:

- Build skills internally
- Recruit externally
- Redeploy existing employees
- Use contractors or external expertise
- Automate certain activities

---

## 5.3 Skills Gap Closure Rate

### Formula

Skills Gaps Closed ÷ Total Identified Skills Gaps × 100

### HRBP Use

Measures the effectiveness of learning, development and talent interventions.

---

# 6. Critical Workforce Metrics

## 6.1 Critical Role

### Definition

A position that has significant importance to business continuity, revenue, compliance, operations, customer service or strategic capability.

### HRBP Use

Critical roles should receive appropriate:

- Succession planning
- Risk assessment
- Talent development
- Retention planning
- Knowledge-transfer planning

---

## 6.2 Critical Role Coverage

### Definition

The percentage of critical positions that have an identified successor.

### Formula

Critical Roles With Identified Successors ÷ Total Critical Roles × 100

### HRBP Use

Measures succession readiness and business-continuity protection.

---

## 6.3 Succession Readiness

### Definition

The degree to which identified successors are prepared to assume critical positions.

Typical categories include:

- Ready Now
- Ready in 1–2 Years
- Ready in 3+ Years
- No Identified Successor

---

# 7. Absence Metrics

## 7.1 Absenteeism Rate

### Formula

Total Days Absent ÷ Total Available Working Days × 100

### HRBP Use

Can help identify:

- Workforce availability issues
- Attendance patterns
- Employee-experience issues
- Operational disruption
- Potential management concerns

---

## 7.2 Absence Frequency

### Definition

The average number of absence events recorded for employees during a specified period.

### HRBP Use

Helps identify recurring attendance patterns.

---

# 8. Compensation Metrics

## 8.1 Average Salary

### Formula

Total Employee Salary ÷ Number of Employees

### HRBP Use

Used for:

- Workforce budgeting
- Compensation analysis
- Salary planning
- Workforce-cost forecasting

---

## 8.2 Payroll Cost

### Definition

The total compensation cost associated with employees over a defined period.

### HRBP Use

Supports:

- Workforce budgeting
- Cost forecasting
- Organizational planning
- Management reporting

---

## 8.3 Salary Range

### Definition

The minimum and maximum compensation within a defined employee population or job structure.

### HRBP Use

Can help identify:

- Pay inconsistencies
- Salary compression
- Salary progression
- Compensation structures

---

## 8.4 Compa-Ratio

### Definition

Compa-ratio compares an employee's salary with the midpoint of the salary range for their role.

### Formula

Employee Salary ÷ Salary Range Midpoint × 100

### HRBP Use

Can support compensation analysis and pay-position discussions.

---

# 9. Employee Engagement Metrics

## 9.1 Employee Engagement Score

### Definition

A measurement of employee connection, commitment, motivation and willingness to contribute to organizational objectives.

### HRBP Use

Can help identify:

- Engagement strengths
- Engagement risks
- Managerial issues
- Employee-experience opportunities

---

## 9.2 Employee Net Promoter Score (eNPS)

### Formula

Percentage of Promoters − Percentage of Detractors

### HRBP Use

Provides an indicator of employee advocacy and employee experience.

---

# 10. Learning & Development Metrics

## 10.1 Training Participation Rate

### Formula

Employees Participating in Training ÷ Eligible Employees × 100

---

## 10.2 Training Completion Rate

### Formula

Employees Completing Training ÷ Employees Enrolled × 100

---

## 10.3 Training Effectiveness

Training effectiveness can be assessed using:

- Knowledge improvement
- Skill application
- Performance improvement
- Manager feedback
- Employee feedback
- Business outcomes

---

## 10.4 Learning Hours per Employee

### Formula

Total Learning Hours ÷ Number of Employees

### HRBP Use

Helps measure organizational investment in employee development.

---

# 11. Workforce Cost Metrics

## 11.1 Workforce Cost per Employee

### Formula

Total Workforce Cost ÷ Average Headcount

### HRBP Use

Helps management understand the average cost associated with maintaining workforce capacity.

---

## 11.2 Revenue per Employee

### Formula

Total Revenue ÷ Average Headcount

### HRBP Use

Provides a high-level indicator of workforce productivity relative to organizational revenue.

This metric should be interpreted alongside business model, industry and organizational structure.

---

# 12. Diversity & Workforce Composition Metrics

## 12.1 Workforce Composition

### Definition

The distribution of employees across relevant workforce characteristics such as:

- Department
- Location
- Employment type
- Job level
- Gender

### HRBP Use

Helps understand workforce structure and representation.

---

## 12.2 Representation Rate

### Formula

Employees in Defined Population ÷ Total Workforce × 100

### HRBP Use

Can be used to monitor workforce representation against organizational objectives and applicable requirements.

---

# 13. HR Service Delivery Metrics

## 13.1 HR Case Resolution Time

### Definition

The average time taken to resolve employee or HR service requests.

### HRBP Use

Measures HR service responsiveness.

---

## 13.2 HR Service Satisfaction

### Definition

Employee satisfaction with HR services received.

### HRBP Use

Helps evaluate the employee experience provided by the HR function.

---

# 14. HRBP Decision Framework

HR metrics should not be viewed in isolation.

A strong HRBP analysis connects multiple indicators to business circumstances.

### Example 1

**High Turnover Risk + Critical Role + Skills Gap**

may indicate a significant workforce continuity risk.

### Example 2

**Workforce Gap + High Recruitment Cost + Internal Skills Availability**

may justify evaluating internal mobility before external recruitment.

### Example 3

**Low Performance + Partial Skills Assessment + Short Tenure**

may indicate a need to review onboarding, role clarity, training and manager support before concluding that the employee is unsuitable.

# 15. Data Interpretation Principles

HR data should be:

- Accurate
- Consistent
- Relevant
- Timely
- Contextualized
- Confidential
- Action-oriented

HR metrics should not automatically be treated as explanations of employee behavior.

A metric identifies a pattern.

The HRBP investigates the underlying causes.

# 16. HR Analytics Maturity Model

## Level 1 — Reporting

"What happened?"

Examples:

- Headcount
- Turnover
- Payroll
- Absence

---

## Level 2 — Diagnostic Analytics

"Why did it happen?"

Examples:

- Turnover by department
- Performance by tenure
- Skills gaps by role
- Absence patterns

---

## Level 3 — Predictive Analytics

"What may happen?"

Examples:

- Turnover-risk modelling
- Workforce demand forecasting
- Succession-risk analysis

---

## Level 4 — Prescriptive Analytics

"What should we do?"

Examples:

- Recruitment prioritization
- Workforce redeployment
- Skills investment
- Retention interventions
- Workforce-cost scenarios

---

# 17. HRBP Analytics Framework

A strong HRBP should move through five stages:

**DATA → INSIGHT → DIAGNOSIS → RECOMMENDATION → ACTION**

### Data

Collect reliable workforce information.

### Insight

Identify meaningful patterns and relationships.

### Diagnosis

Investigate the likely business and people factors behind those patterns.

### Recommendation

Develop evidence-based HR interventions.

### Action

Implement, monitor and measure the intervention.

# 19. Data Governance

HR data is sensitive organizational information and should be managed appropriately.

Good HR data governance includes:

- Access control
- Data accuracy
- Data minimization
- Appropriate retention
- Confidentiality
- Consistent definitions
- Auditability
- Secure storage

Only appropriate and authorized information should be included in HR analytics systems and portfolios.

# Conclusion

HR analytics becomes valuable when data is translated into business insight and actionable people strategy.

The HR Business Partner's role is to move beyond reporting numbers and help leadership understand:

- What is happening?
- Why is it happening?
- What business risk does it create?
- What options are available?
- What should be measured after action is taken?

The ultimate HRBP analytics framework is:

**Data → Insight → Diagnosis → Recommendation → Action → Measurement**
