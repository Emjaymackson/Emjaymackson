# HR Metrics and KPI Library

## Overview

HR metrics provide structured measurements for understanding workforce performance and organizational trends.

Metrics should always be connected to a clear business question.

---

## 1. Headcount

**Definition:** Number of employees within a defined population and period.

**Use:**

* Workforce planning
* Organizational reporting
* Budget planning

---

## 2. Headcount Growth

**Formula:**

```text
(Current Headcount - Previous Headcount)
÷ Previous Headcount × 100
```

---

## 3. Turnover Rate

A commonly used calculation is:

```text
Number of Separations
÷ Average Headcount × 100
```

The organization should define whether the calculation includes voluntary, involuntary, or all separations.

---

## 4. Retention Rate

A commonly used approach:

```text
Employees Remaining During Period
÷ Employees at Beginning of Period × 100
```

The exact methodology should be documented.

---

## 5. Time to Fill

**Definition:** Time taken to fill an approved vacancy.

Possible measurement:

```text
Date Position Filled - Date Vacancy Approved
```

Useful for:

* Recruitment efficiency
* Workforce planning
* Hiring manager service levels

---

## 6. Time to Hire

Measures the time between a candidate entering the recruitment process and accepting an offer, depending on the organization's definition.

---

## 7. Offer Acceptance Rate

```text
Offers Accepted
÷ Offers Made × 100
```

---

## 8. Cost per Hire

A basic formula:

```text
Total Recruitment Costs
÷ Number of Hires
```

Costs may include:

* Advertising
* Recruitment technology
* Agency fees
* Assessment
* Background checks
* Recruitment events

---

## 9. Absence Rate

```text
Total Absence Days
÷ Total Scheduled Working Days × 100
```

---

## 10. Training Completion Rate

```text
Employees Who Completed Training
÷ Employees Required to Complete Training × 100
```

Completion does not necessarily demonstrate learning effectiveness.

---

## 11. Internal Mobility Rate

```text
Employees Who Moved Internally
÷ Average Headcount × 100
```

---

## 12. Promotion Rate

```text
Employees Promoted During Period
÷ Eligible Employee Population × 100
```

---

## 13. Employee Engagement Score

Usually derived from a defined employee survey methodology.

Important considerations:

* Response rate
* Question design
* Population
* Survey period
* Scale
* Statistical interpretation

---

## 14. Performance Distribution

Can be analysed by:

* Rating
* Department
* Role
* Manager
* Tenure
* Business unit

Performance distributions should be interpreted alongside calibration and role context.

---

## 15. Employee Relations Metrics

Examples:

* Number of cases
* Cases by category
* Cases by department
* Average case resolution time
* Repeat cases
* Grievance volume
* Disciplinary cases
* Investigation duration

---

## 16. Compensation Metrics

Examples:

* Salary range penetration
* Compa-ratio
* Payroll cost
* Compensation by grade
* Compensation by department
* Overtime cost

### Compa-Ratio

```text
Employee Salary
÷ Salary Range Midpoint × 100
```

---

## 17. Quality of Hire

Possible indicators include:

* New-hire performance
* Retention after defined period
* Hiring manager satisfaction
* Time to productivity
* Probation outcomes

Quality of hire should be defined clearly before measurement.

---

## 18. Metric Governance

Every KPI should have:

* Definition
* Formula
* Data source
* Reporting frequency
* Owner
* Population
* Measurement period
* Interpretation notes

---

## 19. Metric Interpretation Rule

A metric should not be interpreted in isolation.

For example:

> A rise in turnover does not automatically mean compensation is the cause.

HR should examine:

* Exit reasons
* Tenure
* Department
* Role
* Manager
* Market conditions
* Workload
* Career opportunities
* Compensation
* Employee feedback

---

## 20. HRBP Principle

The objective of HR analytics is not to produce more numbers.

The objective is to use reliable evidence to improve workforce decisions.
