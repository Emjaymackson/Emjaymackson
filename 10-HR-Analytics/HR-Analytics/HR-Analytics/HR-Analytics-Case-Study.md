# HR Analytics Case Study

## Fictional Case Study: Rising Employee Turnover

### Organization

**Company:** Meridian Business Services
**Industry:** Professional Services
**Employees:** 250

This is a fictionalized portfolio scenario.

---

## 1. Business Problem

Management observed an increase in employee resignations over a six-month period.

The business wanted HR to determine:

* Where turnover was occurring.
* Which employee groups were most affected.
* Possible contributing factors.
* What actions could be considered.

---

## 2. Initial Data

| Metric         | Previous Period | Current Period |
| -------------- | --------------: | -------------: |
| Headcount      |             235 |            250 |
| Separations    |               9 |             17 |
| Open Positions |               8 |             14 |
| Average Tenure |         3.4 yrs |        3.1 yrs |
| Absence Rate   |            2.8% |           3.5% |

The figures are illustrative.

---

## 3. Analysis

The HRBP segmented turnover by:

* Department
* Tenure
* Role
* Exit reason
* Manager
* Employee level

---

## 4. Observations

The analysis identified that:

1. A significant proportion of resignations came from employees with less than two years of service.
2. Certain operational roles had higher turnover than other functions.
3. Exit feedback frequently referenced workload and career progression.
4. Recruitment activity increased as vacancies remained open longer.
5. Absence also increased during the same period.

These observations indicate areas for further investigation rather than proof of a single cause.

---

## 5. Root-Cause Investigation

Potential contributing factors were investigated through:

* Exit interviews
* Employee discussions
* Manager feedback
* Workload review
* Career progression analysis
* Compensation review
* Recruitment data

---

## 6. Possible Contributing Factors

The fictional analysis suggested several areas requiring management attention:

### Career Development

Employees reported limited visibility into career progression.

### Workload

Certain teams experienced increased workload because vacancies remained open.

### Management Practices

Employee feedback suggested differences in management experience across teams.

### Recruitment Delays

Open positions increased workload pressure on existing employees.

---

## 7. Recommended HR Actions

Potential interventions included:

### Immediate

* Review critical vacancies.
* Prioritize recruitment for high-pressure functions.
* Conduct manager check-ins.
* Review workload distribution.

### Medium Term

* Introduce career development discussions.
* Strengthen manager capability.
* Review internal mobility opportunities.
* Improve workforce planning.

### Long Term

* Develop succession planning.
* Strengthen employee listening.
* Review workforce structure.
* Establish recurring turnover analytics.

---

| Time to Fill       | Monthly           |

| Internal Mobility  | Quarterly         |
| Exit Reasons       | Monthly/Quarterly |

---

## 9. HRBP Business Partnership

The HRBP should present the issue in business language:

> Workforce turnover is increasing in specific employee populations. The available data indicates several contributing factors that require targeted investigation rather than a single broad intervention.

This allows leadership to focus resources on evidence-supported areas.

---

## 10. Outcome Measurement

After interventions are introduced, the organization should compare:

* Turnover before and after intervention
* Vacancy duration
* Absence
* Engagement
* Employee feedback
* Internal mobility
* New-hire retention
