# HR Analytics Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework provides a structured approach for using workforce data to support HR and business decisions.

The framework helps HR professionals move from basic reporting to meaningful workforce insight.

---

## 2. Analytics Maturity

### Level 1 — Reporting

Answers:

> What happened?

Examples:

* Current headcount
* Number of hires
* Number of resignations
* Training attendance

### Level 2 — Descriptive Analytics

Identifies patterns and trends.

Examples:

* Monthly turnover trend
* Absence by department
* Recruitment volume by role

### Level 3 — Diagnostic Analytics

Explores:

> Why might this be happening?

Examples:

* Exit reasons by department
* Turnover by tenure
* Performance trends by manager or business unit

Diagnostic analysis should distinguish between correlation and causation.

### Level 4 — Predictive Analytics

Uses historical information and statistical methods to estimate possible future outcomes.

Examples:

* Potential workforce demand
* Recruitment demand
* Attrition risk modelling

Predictive analysis should be treated as an estimate rather than certainty.

### Level 5 — Decision Support

Connects analysis to practical management decisions.

Examples:

* Workforce restructuring
* Recruitment planning
* Capability development
* Retention interventions
* Compensation reviews

---

## 3. Analytics Workflow

```text
1. Define the business problem
2. Define the population
3. Define the measurement period
4. Identify data sources
5. Validate the data
6. Clean the dataset
7. Calculate metrics
8. Segment the information
9. Identify trends
10. Investigate possible causes
11. Develop insights
12. Recommend actions
13. Measure results
```

---

## 4. Business Question Framework

Before analysing data, clarify:

### Business Problem

What business problem are we trying to understand?

### Workforce Population

Which employees or groups are being analysed?

### Time Period

What period does the data cover?

### Metrics

Which measurements are relevant?

### Comparison

What should the result be compared against?

Possible comparisons include:

* Previous period
* Budget
* Target
* Department
* Location
* Employee group
* Historical average

---

## 5. Data Sources

Potential HR data sources include:

* HRIS
* Payroll
* Recruitment systems
* ATS
* Attendance records
* Performance records
* Engagement surveys
* Training records
* Employee relations records
* Exit interviews
* Workforce planning data

---

## 6. Segmentation

Useful segmentation can include:

* Department
* Role
* Location
* Tenure
* Grade
* Employment type
* Gender where legally and operationally appropriate
* Age group where appropriate and lawful
* Manager
* Recruitment source

Segmentation should have a legitimate business purpose and should not create unnecessary privacy risks.

---

## 7. Trend Analysis

Examples:

```text
Monthly Turnover
January     2.1%
February    2.8%
March       3.4%
April       4.1%
May         4.5%
```

A trend does not automatically establish its cause.

Further investigation may be required.

---

## 8. Root-Cause Analysis

Useful techniques include:

### 5 Whys

Repeatedly ask why a problem is occurring until the underlying contributing factors become clearer.

### Fishbone Analysis

Potential categories:

* People
* Process
* Technology
* Leadership
* Policy
* Work Environment
* Compensation
* Capability

### Employee Feedback

Use:

* Surveys
* Interviews
* Focus groups
* Exit interviews
* Manager feedback

---

## 9. Data Storytelling

A strong HR analytics presentation should answer:

1. What is happening?
2. Why does it matter?
3. Where is it happening?
4. What evidence supports the observation?
5. What are the possible contributing factors?
6. What should management consider doing?
7. How will success be measured?

## 11. Analytics Governance

HR analytics should consider:

* Data accuracy
* Data privacy
* Access control
* Data retention
* Metric definitions
* Reporting consistency
* Appropriate aggregation
* Confidentiality
* Documentation

---

## 12. Portfolio Application

Each analytics project in this portfolio should demonstrate:

**Business Problem → Data → Analysis → Insight → Recommendation → Action → Measurement**

This ensures that the portfolio demonstrates business partnering rather than reporting alone.
