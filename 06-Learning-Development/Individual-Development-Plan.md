# Individual Development Plan

## 1. Purpose

The Individual Development Plan (IDP) provides a structured framework for employees and managers to identify development priorities, build relevant capabilities, and support career growth.

The IDP connects individual development needs with role expectations, business priorities, career aspirations, and future capability requirements.

The HR Business Partner (HRBP) uses IDPs to support career development, capability building, succession planning, internal mobility, and employee engagement.

## 3. Current Role Assessment

### Current Responsibilities

List the employee's major responsibilities and expected outcomes.

### Current Strengths

Identify capabilities where the employee currently demonstrates strong performance.

Examples:

- Technical expertise
- Communication
- Leadership
- Problem-solving
- Customer management
- Collaboration
- Commercial awareness

### Development Areas

Identify capabilities that require improvement or further development.

Development needs should be supported by evidence such as:

- Performance reviews
- Capability assessments
- Manager feedback
- Employee self-assessment
- 360-degree feedback
- Career aspirations
- Business requirements

---

## 4. Career Aspirations

The employee and manager should discuss the employee's short- and longer-term career interests.

### Short-Term Career Goal

What does the employee want to achieve within the next 12–24 months?

### Longer-Term Career Direction

What type of role, responsibility, or expertise does the employee want to develop toward?

### Career Interests

Consider:

- Leadership
- Technical specialization
- People management
- Project management
- Cross-functional roles
- International opportunities
- Specialist career paths

Career aspirations should be discussed realistically alongside available organizational opportunities and capability requirements.

## 9. 70-20-10 Development Approach

Where appropriate, development can be structured around a blended approach:

### 70% – Experiential Learning

- Stretch assignments
- Projects
- Job rotations
- Acting roles
- New responsibilities

### 20% – Social Learning

- Coaching
- Mentoring
- Peer learning
- Manager feedback
- Communities of practice

### 10% – Formal Learning

- Courses
- Workshops
- Certifications
- E-learning
- Structured training

The 70-20-10 model should be treated as a development guideline rather than a mandatory allocation.

## 10. Manager Support

The manager should help create the conditions required for development.

Manager responsibilities include:

- Discussing development goals
- Providing regular feedback
- Assigning appropriate development opportunities
- Supporting learning participation
- Providing coaching
- Creating opportunities to apply new skills
- Reviewing progress
- Addressing barriers to development

---

## 11. Employee Responsibilities

The employee is responsible for:

- Taking ownership of development goals
- Participating actively in agreed development activities
- Applying new knowledge and skills
- Seeking feedback
- Tracking progress
- Raising development challenges
- Discussing career aspirations with the manager
- Updating the IDP when circumstances change

---

## 12. HRBP / L&D Responsibilities

The HRBP or L&D team should:

- Provide the IDP framework
- Support managers and employees
- Connect development needs to organizational capability priorities
- Identify relevant learning opportunities
- Support career development
- Monitor development trends
- Connect IDPs with succession planning where appropriate
- Maintain appropriate development records

## 14. Progress Review

IDPs should be reviewed periodically rather than only at the end of the performance cycle.

Suggested review points:

- Quarterly
- Mid-year
- Annual performance review
- Following major role changes
- Before promotion discussions
- During succession planning

Progress should be assessed using evidence rather than completion alone.

---

## 15. Development Success Measures

Success measures may include:

- Improved capability assessment
- Successful completion of certification
- Improved performance KPI
- Successful project delivery
- Increased responsibility
- Demonstrated new behavior
- Improved stakeholder feedback
- Successful internal move
- Promotion readiness
- Increased role proficiency

---

## 16. Barriers to Development

Potential barriers should be documented and addressed.

Examples include:

- Workload
- Limited access to learning
- Budget constraints
- Lack of manager support
- Limited internal opportunities
- Scheduling conflicts
- Missing prerequisites
- Operational constraints

Where a development goal cannot be achieved as originally planned, the IDP should be adjusted rather than simply marked as incomplete.

---

---

## 18. Connection to Succession Planning

For employees identified as potential successors to critical roles, the IDP may include targeted development actions such as:

- Leadership assignments
- Cross-functional projects
- Business-critical projects
- Executive mentoring
- Acting responsibilities
- Strategic exposure
- Technical specialization

Succession-related development information should be handled confidentially and shared only with authorized stakeholders.

---

## 19. Confidentiality

Individual development information should be treated appropriately.

The organization should:

- Protect employee development records
- Restrict access to authorized personnel
- Avoid publicly identifying employees in portfolio examples
- Use fictional or anonymized examples in public repositories
- Keep sensitive performance and career information confidential
