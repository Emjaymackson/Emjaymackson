# Training Needs Analysis Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework establishes a structured approach for identifying employee training and development needs based on business priorities, role requirements, performance expectations, and capability gaps.

The HR Business Partner (HRBP) uses Training Needs Analysis (TNA) to determine where learning interventions can improve employee capability and business performance, while distinguishing training needs from issues that require non-training solutions.

---

## 2. Objectives

The Training Needs Analysis process aims to:

- Identify current and future capability requirements
- Identify gaps between required and current capability
- Determine whether training is an appropriate solution
- Prioritize critical learning needs
- Align training investments with business objectives
- Support individual development planning
- Inform annual learning and development plans
- Improve the effectiveness of training investments
- Provide evidence for learning-budget decisions

---

## 3. Guiding Principles

TNA should be:

- **Business-Aligned** – Connected to organizational and departmental priorities
- **Evidence-Based** – Supported by performance, capability, and workforce data
- **Role-Specific** – Based on actual job requirements
- **Needs-Driven** – Focused on genuine capability gaps
- **Practical** – Designed around real workplace requirements
- **Prioritized** – Focused on the gaps with the greatest business impact
- **Inclusive** – Incorporating relevant input from employees, managers, and other stakeholders
- **Measurable** – Linked to defined learning and performance outcomes

---

## 4. Levels of Training Needs Analysis

TNA should be conducted at three primary levels.

### 4.1 Organizational Analysis

Organizational analysis examines the capabilities required to achieve business objectives.

Areas to consider include:

- Business strategy
- Organizational goals
- Workforce plans
- Business performance
- New products or services
- Technology changes
- Organizational restructuring
- Regulatory requirements
- Customer expectations
- Future skill requirements

### 4.2 Job / Task Analysis

Job analysis identifies the capabilities required to perform a specific role effectively.

Areas include:

- Key responsibilities
- Required knowledge
- Technical skills
- Behavioral competencies
- Decision-making requirements
- Tools and systems
- Required qualifications
- Performance standards
- Critical tasks

### 4.3 Individual Analysis

Individual analysis identifies capability gaps for specific employees or employee groups.

Evidence may include:

- Performance reviews
- Skills assessments
- Manager observations
- Employee self-assessments
- 360-degree feedback
- Productivity data
- Quality data
- Customer feedback
- Career-development discussions

## 6. Training Need vs. Performance Problem

Not every performance problem is a training problem.

Before recommending training, the HRBP and manager should determine the root cause.

Potential causes include:

### Capability Issue

The employee does not have the required knowledge or skill.

**Potential solution:** Training, coaching, mentoring, or practical development.

### Process Issue

The employee understands the work but the process is unclear or inefficient.

**Potential solution:** Process redesign or clearer procedures.

### Resource Issue

The employee lacks the tools, systems, staffing, or resources required.

**Potential solution:** Resource or operational intervention.

### Motivation / Accountability Issue

The employee has the capability but is not consistently applying it.

**Potential solution:** Performance management, coaching, accountability, or management intervention.

### Role Clarity Issue

Expectations or responsibilities are unclear.

**Potential solution:** Clarification of role, objectives, responsibilities, or performance standards.

This distinction helps prevent unnecessary training expenditure.

## 7. TNA Process

### Step 1: Understand Business Priorities

Review current and future business objectives.

Questions include:

- What is the organization trying to achieve?
- What capabilities are required?
- What skills will become more important?
- What business risks could capability gaps create?

### Step 2: Identify Required Capability

Define the knowledge, skills, and behaviors required to meet performance expectations.

### Step 3: Assess Current Capability

Review available evidence to determine current capability levels.

### Step 4: Identify the Gap

Compare required capability against current capability.

**Capability Gap = Required Capability − Current Capability**

### Step 5: Determine Root Cause

Establish whether the gap is genuinely caused by a learning or capability issue.

### Step 6: Prioritize Needs

Rank identified needs according to business impact, urgency, risk, and population affected.

### Step 7: Determine Learning Intervention

Select the most appropriate development solution.

Potential solutions include:

- Formal training
- E-learning
- Coaching
- Mentoring
- Job rotation
- Stretch assignments
- Workshops
- Professional certification
- Peer learning
- On-the-job training

### Step 8: Define Success Measures

Establish how the organization will determine whether the intervention was effective.

### Step 9: Build the Training Plan

Translate prioritized needs into an approved learning plan.

### Step 10: Evaluate Outcomes

Review learning, behavior, and business outcomes after implementation.

## 8. TNA Prioritization Matrix

Training needs can be prioritized using a simple assessment framework.

| Factor | Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business Impact | Limited | Moderate | Significant |
| Urgency | Can Wait | Within Planning Cycle | Immediate |
| Risk | Low | Moderate | High |
| Population Affected | Small Group | Department | Organization |
| Capability Gap | Minor | Moderate | Significant |

High-impact and high-risk gaps should receive greater attention when resources are limited.

## 10. Methods of Data Collection

The HRBP may use multiple methods to gather information.

### Interviews

Structured discussions with:

- Senior leaders
- Managers
- Employees
- Subject-matter experts

### Surveys

Questionnaires can be used to collect information from larger employee populations.

### Observation

Direct observation may help identify practical skill gaps.

### Performance Data

Review:

- Productivity
- Quality
- Error rates
- Customer complaints
- Sales results
- Service levels
- Other relevant KPIs

### Assessments

Examples include:

- Skills tests
- Knowledge assessments
- Competency assessments
- Simulations
- Role-specific assessments

### Document Review

Relevant documents may include:

- Job descriptions
- Performance standards
- Business plans
- Audit findings
- Customer feedback
- Succession plans
- Workforce plans

---

## 11. Training Audience Segmentation

Training needs should be grouped according to the affected population.

Possible segments include:

- New employees
- Existing employees
- First-line managers
- Middle managers
- Senior leaders
- High-potential employees
- Critical-role employees
- Specific job families
- Specific departments

This allows learning interventions to be designed around the actual needs of each group.

---

## 12. Individual Training Needs

Individual training requirements may be identified through:

- Performance reviews
- Development discussions
- Career goals
- Capability assessments
- Manager recommendations
- Employee requests
- Promotion preparation
- Succession planning

Individual needs should be incorporated into the employee's Individual Development Plan (IDP) where appropriate.

---

## 13. Mandatory Training

Certain training may be required because of:

- Legal obligations
- Regulatory requirements
- Health and safety
- Data protection
- Information security
- Industry standards
- Internal policies

Mandatory training should be tracked separately from discretionary development programs.

---

## 14. HRBP Responsibilities

The HRBP should:

- Facilitate organizational and departmental TNA
- Partner with managers to identify capability gaps
- Analyze performance and workforce data
- Challenge unsupported training requests
- Identify root causes of performance problems
- Prioritize critical capability gaps
- Connect TNA findings to the L&D strategy
- Support training-budget decisions
- Monitor learning outcomes
- Provide recommendations to leadership

---

## 15. Manager Responsibilities

Managers should:

- Identify role-specific capability requirements
- Provide accurate performance information
- Discuss development needs with employees
- Participate in needs analysis
- Support development activities
- Provide opportunities to apply learning
- Monitor post-training performance

---

## 16. TNA and Workforce Planning

TNA should also consider future workforce requirements.

Workforce planning may identify:

- New roles
- Emerging skills
- Skills shortages
- Leadership gaps
- Succession risks
- Technology-related capability requirements

These findings should feed into the organization's longer-term learning strategy.

---

## 17. Measuring TNA Effectiveness

The effectiveness of the TNA process can be monitored through:

- Percentage of training requests supported by documented needs
- Percentage of identified gaps with development plans
- Training-plan alignment with business priorities
- Post-training performance improvement
- Reduction in recurring capability-related issues
- Manager satisfaction with training relevance
- Employee development-plan completion
- Learning investment allocated to critical capability gaps

---

## 18. Documentation & Confidentiality

TNA information should be managed securely.

The organization should:

- Protect employee assessment information
- Limit access to authorized personnel
- Use aggregated data for organizational reporting
- Keep identifiable employee information out of public repositories
- Use fictional or anonymized examples in portfolio materials
