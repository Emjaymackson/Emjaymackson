# Training Delivery Framework

## 1. Purpose

The Training Delivery Framework provides a structured approach for planning, delivering, managing, and evaluating employee training programs.

The framework ensures that training is delivered through appropriate methods, reaches the intended audience, supports practical application, and contributes to measurable capability improvement.

---

## 2. Objectives

The framework aims to:

* Establish consistent training delivery standards
* Match delivery methods to learning objectives
* Improve learner participation and engagement
* Support practical application of learning
* Provide flexibility across different employee groups
* Improve learning transfer to the workplace
* Establish clear responsibilities for training delivery
* Support measurement of training effectiveness

---

## 3. Training Delivery Principles

Training should be:

* Relevant to business and role requirements
* Practical and application-focused
* Appropriate for the target audience
* Accessible to participants
* Interactive where appropriate
* Supported by qualified facilitators
* Measurable
* Consistent with organizational policies
* Continuously improved based on evidence and feedback

---

## 4. Training Delivery Methods

### 4.1 Instructor-Led Training

Facilitator-led sessions conducted physically or virtually.

Suitable for:

* Technical training
* Workshops
* Leadership development
* Compliance training
* Team development
* Complex subjects requiring discussion

### 4.2 Virtual Training

Training delivered through digital communication and collaboration platforms.

Suitable for:

* Distributed teams
* Remote employees
* Knowledge-based training
* Short learning sessions
* Cross-location programs

### 4.3 E-Learning

Self-paced digital learning completed through a learning platform or approved digital resources.

Suitable for:

* Mandatory learning
* Policy awareness
* Basic knowledge development
* Refresher training
* Scalable learning programs

### 4.4 On-the-Job Training

Learning that occurs while employees perform actual work activities.

Examples include:

* Job shadowing
* Demonstrations
* Guided practice
* Work assignments
* Process training

### 4.5 Coaching

A manager, HR professional, or qualified coach supports an employee in improving a specific capability or performance area.

### 4.6 Mentoring

A more experienced employee provides guidance, knowledge, and career support to a less experienced employee.

### 4.7 Workshops

Interactive sessions focused on application, problem-solving, discussion, and collaboration.

### 4.8 Job Rotation

Employees gain experience by temporarily performing responsibilities in another role or function.

### 4.9 Blended Learning

A combination of multiple learning methods.

Example:

**E-learning → Classroom Workshop → Practical Assignment → Coaching → Evaluation**

---

## 5. Selecting the Appropriate Delivery Method

The delivery method should be selected based on:

* Learning objectives
* Complexity of the subject
* Target audience
* Number of participants
* Geographic distribution
* Available technology
* Budget
* Required interaction
* Need for practical application
* Time available
* Confidentiality requirements

---

## 6. Delivery Method Selection Matrix

| Learning Requirement           | Recommended Method               |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| Basic policy awareness         | E-learning / Virtual             |
| Technical demonstration        | Instructor-led / On-the-job      |
| Leadership development         | Workshop / Coaching / Mentoring  |
| Practical skill development    | On-the-job / Workshop            |
| Compliance awareness           | E-learning / Instructor-led      |
| Team development               | Workshop / Facilitated session   |
| Career development             | Coaching / Mentoring             |
| Complex problem-solving        | Workshop / Experiential learning |
| Large-scale learning           | E-learning / Virtual             |
| Role-specific process training | On-the-job / Instructor-led      |

The recommended method should always be adjusted to the actual learning objective.

---

## 7. Training Delivery Process

### Step 1: Confirm Training Requirement

Validate the business need and learning requirement through the Training Needs Analysis process.

### Step 2: Define Learning Objectives

Clearly state what participants should know, understand, or demonstrate after the training.

### Step 3: Identify Participants

Define the target audience based on role, capability, business need, or compliance requirement.

### Step 4: Select Delivery Method

Determine the most appropriate delivery method based on the learning requirement.

### Step 5: Select Facilitator or Provider

Identify an appropriately qualified internal facilitator, subject matter expert, or external training provider.

### Step 6: Develop Training Materials

Prepare relevant materials such as:

* Presentation slides
* Participant guides
* Workbooks
* Case studies
* Exercises
* Assessments
* Job aids
* Reference materials

### Step 7: Schedule Training

Confirm:

* Date
* Time
* Venue or virtual platform
* Duration
* Participants
* Facilitator
* Required equipment
* Learning materials

### Step 8: Communicate Training

Provide participants with appropriate information before the session.

### Step 9: Deliver Training

Conduct the training according to the approved learning plan.

### Step 10: Assess Learning

Use appropriate assessment methods to determine whether learning objectives were achieved.

### Step 11: Support Learning Transfer

Provide opportunities for participants to apply learning in their actual work.

### Step 12: Evaluate

Measure participant experience, learning, application, and relevant business outcomes.

---

## 8. Pre-Training Preparation

Participants should receive relevant information before training.

This may include:

* Training objectives
* Schedule
* Venue or access link
* Required preparation
* Pre-reading
* Pre-assessment
* Required materials
* Expected participation standards

Managers should ensure that employees have sufficient time and support to participate.

---

## 9. Facilitator Standards

Facilitators should demonstrate:

* Subject knowledge
* Communication ability
* Facilitation skills
* Understanding of the target audience
* Ability to manage discussions
* Ability to encourage participation
* Ability to explain practical applications
* Professional conduct

External facilitators should be evaluated against agreed provider requirements.

---

## 10. Training Session Structure

A standard training session may follow this structure:

1. Welcome and introduction
2. Learning objectives
3. Participant expectations
4. Knowledge or experience assessment
5. Core learning content
6. Demonstration or explanation
7. Practical activity
8. Discussion
9. Knowledge assessment
10. Workplace application
11. Questions and clarification
12. Summary and key takeaways
13. Evaluation
14. Next steps

The structure may be adapted depending on the subject and audience.

---

## 11. Learner Engagement

Training should encourage active participation.

Possible techniques include:

* Group discussions
* Case studies
* Role plays
* Simulations
* Practical exercises
* Question-and-answer sessions
* Peer learning
* Problem-solving activities
* Demonstrations
* Reflection exercises

The appropriate technique should depend on the learning objective.

---

## 12. Training Attendance

Training attendance should be documented where appropriate.

Records may include:

* Employee name
* Employee ID
* Department
* Training title
* Training date
* Attendance status
* Completion status
* Assessment result

For mandatory training, completion should be monitored and outstanding participants followed up.

---

## 13. Learning Assessment

Assessment methods may include:

### Knowledge Assessment

* Quizzes
* Tests
* Written assessments

### Practical Assessment

* Demonstrations
* Simulations
* Work assignments
* Practical exercises

### Behavioral Assessment

* Manager observation
* Feedback
* Behavioral checklists

### Application Assessment

Evaluation of whether employees are applying the learning in their roles.

---

## 14. Learning Transfer

Training is not considered fully effective simply because an employee attended the session.

Learning transfer should be supported through:

* Manager follow-up
* Practical assignments
* Coaching
* Job aids
* Reinforcement sessions
* Refresher learning
* Workplace application
* Feedback

Managers should discuss how employees can apply the learning to their actual responsibilities.

---

## 15. Post-Training Follow-Up

Post-training activities may include:

* Manager check-ins
* Application assignments
* Coaching sessions
* Knowledge refreshers
* Follow-up assessments
* Observation
* Performance reviews
* Peer learning sessions

The follow-up period should reflect the complexity of the learning objective.

---

## 16. Training Evaluation

Training evaluation should consider multiple levels.

### Level 1: Participant Reaction

Measures participant perception of:

* Relevance
* Quality
* Facilitator effectiveness
* Materials
* Learning environment

### Level 2: Learning

Measures whether participants gained the intended knowledge or skills.

### Level 3: Application

Measures whether participants apply the learning in their roles.

### Level 4: Business Impact

Measures whether the learning contributes to relevant business outcomes.

---

## 17. Training Delivery Risks

| Risk                     | Potential Impact           | Mitigation                  |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Low attendance           | Reduced learning coverage  | Manager follow-up           |
| Poor facilitator quality | Low learning effectiveness | Facilitator assessment      |
| Irrelevant content       | Low engagement             | Needs analysis              |
| Technical failure        | Disrupted training         | Pre-session testing         |
| Poor scheduling          | Attendance problems        | Business-aligned scheduling |
| Lack of manager support  | Low application            | Manager involvement         |
| Inadequate materials     | Poor learning experience   | Quality review              |
| No follow-up             | Weak learning transfer     | Post-training reinforcement |

---

## 18. Roles and Responsibilities

### HRBP

The HRBP should:

* Connect training with business priorities
* Identify capability requirements
* Support Training Needs Analysis
* Advise managers on appropriate interventions
* Monitor training outcomes
* Evaluate business impact
* Identify improvement opportunities

### L&D / HR Team

Responsible for:

* Curriculum management
* Training coordination
* Provider management
* Training records
* Evaluation
* Learning resources
* Reporting

### Managers

Managers should:

* Identify team learning needs
* Release employees for training
* Reinforce learning
* Provide opportunities for application
* Monitor employee development

### Employees

Employees should:

* Participate actively
* Complete assigned learning
* Apply new knowledge
* Complete assessments
* Seek feedback
* Take responsibility for development

### Facilitators

Facilitators should:

* Prepare adequately
* Deliver the approved content
* Encourage participation
* Assess learning
* Provide relevant feedback
* Maintain professional standards

---

## 19. Training Technology

Technology may support:

* Virtual classrooms
* Learning management systems
* E-learning
* Attendance tracking
* Digital assessments
* Learning records
* Training dashboards
* Feedback collection
* Automated reminders

Technology should be selected based on organizational needs, accessibility, security, and cost.

## 20. Training Documentation

Training documentation may include:

* Training request
* Training needs analysis
* Training plan
* Participant list
* Attendance record
* Training materials
* Assessment results
* Evaluation results
* Completion records
* Follow-up reports

---

## 21. Training Metrics

Key training delivery metrics may include:

* Training attendance rate
* Training completion rate
* Learning assessment score
* Participant satisfaction
* Training hours per employee
* Mandatory training completion
* Training application rate
* Capability improvement
* Training cost per participant
* Training cost per employee
* Internal promotion readiness
* Performance improvement following training

---

## 22. Continuous Improvement

Training delivery should be continuously reviewed.

Improvement inputs may include:

* Participant feedback
* Facilitator feedback
* Manager feedback
* Assessment results
* Application data
* Business performance data
* Training costs
* Attendance patterns

Where evidence indicates that a training program is not achieving its intended objectives, the content, delivery method, audience, facilitator, or intervention should be reviewed.

---

## 23. Confidentiality & Data Protection

Training records should be handled securely.

The organization should:

* Restrict access to authorized personnel
* Protect employee assessment records
* Avoid exposing individual employee information unnecessarily
* Use anonymized data for reporting where appropriate
* Protect confidential business information
* Follow applicable data protection requirements
