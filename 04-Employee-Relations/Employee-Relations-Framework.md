# Employee Relations Framework

## Overview

Employee relations is the structured management of the relationship between employees, managers and the organization.

An effective HRBP approach seeks to maintain a productive workplace while ensuring that employee concerns, workplace conflicts, conduct issues and organizational risks are handled consistently and appropriately.

---

# 1. Employee Relations Objectives

The framework is designed to:

- Promote a professional workplace
- Support constructive employee-management relationships
- Resolve workplace concerns promptly
- Reduce avoidable conflict
- Promote consistent policy application
- Support managers in handling employee issues
- Identify organizational risks
- Improve employee experience
- Maintain appropriate documentation
- Support business continuity

---

# 2. Core Employee Relations Framework

The process follows:

**Identify → Understand → Investigate → Assess → Act → Document → Monitor**

---

# 3. Types of Employee Relations Matters

Employee-relations cases may include:

### Workplace Conflict

Disagreements between employees, teams or managers.

### Grievances

Formal or informal complaints concerning workplace treatment, decisions, conditions or practices.

### Conduct Issues

Alleged breaches of organizational policies, procedures or expected workplace standards.

### Performance Concerns

Situations where an employee's performance does not meet established expectations.

### Attendance Issues

Repeated or unexplained attendance or punctuality concerns.

### Workplace Behaviour

Concerns involving communication, professionalism, harassment, bullying or inappropriate conduct.

### Manager-Employee Relationship Issues

Breakdowns in communication, expectations or working relationships.

### Policy Concerns

Questions or complaints relating to the interpretation or application of organizational policies.

---

# 4. HRBP Case Assessment

Before recommending action, the HRBP should establish:

- What happened?
- Who is involved?
- When did it happen?
- What evidence exists?
- Which policy or procedure applies?
- Has the issue occurred previously?
- Are there witnesses?
- Has the employee provided an explanation?
- What is the business impact?
- What is the employee impact?
- What risks exist?

---

# 5. Employee Relations Case Classification

Cases can be classified according to their nature and urgency.

| Category | Example |
|---|---|
| Performance | Failure to meet agreed objectives |
| Conduct | Breach of workplace standards |
| Attendance | Repeated lateness |
| Grievance | Employee complaint |
| Conflict | Workplace disagreement |
| Policy | Dispute regarding policy application |
| Managerial | Breakdown between manager and employee |
| Workplace Behaviour | Alleged inappropriate conduct |

---

# 6. Case Severity

A practical internal classification can be:

### Level 1 — Routine

Low-impact employee-relations matter that can normally be handled through manager intervention or informal resolution.

### Level 2 — Moderate

Matter requiring HRBP involvement, documented action or structured intervention.

### Level 3 — Serious

Matter involving significant employee, operational or organizational risk.

### Level 4 — Critical

Matter requiring immediate escalation to appropriate senior management, legal or other specialist support.

Severity should be determined from the facts and applicable organizational policies.

---

# 7. Investigation Principles

Where investigation is appropriate, the HRBP should seek to establish facts before recommending an outcome.

The process should consider:

- Relevant documents
- Employee statements
- Manager statements
- Witness information
- Attendance records
- Communications
- Policies
- Previous documented cases
- Other relevant evidence

The investigator should distinguish between:

**Facts → Evidence → Allegations → Conclusions**

An allegation should not automatically be treated as an established fact.

---

# 8. Employee Opportunity to Respond

Where an allegation or concern could materially affect an employee, the employee should ordinarily have an appropriate opportunity to provide an explanation, subject to applicable law, organizational policy and the circumstances of the case.

The employee's response should be documented appropriately.

---

# 9. HRBP Role

The HRBP should:

- Advise management
- Facilitate fair processes
- Review relevant policy
- Help establish facts
- Identify organizational risk
- Support documentation
- Promote consistency
- Recommend appropriate interventions
- Monitor recurring issues
- Identify systemic causes

The HRBP should avoid becoming an automatic advocate for either management or the employee.

The objective is a fair, evidence-based and business-aware process.

---

# 10. Manager Responsibilities

Managers should:

- Address issues promptly
- Communicate expectations clearly
- Document relevant facts
- Provide feedback
- Apply policies consistently
- Escalate appropriate matters to HR
- Participate in investigations when required
- Follow agreed actions

Managers should not make unsupported allegations or impose disciplinary outcomes outside their authority.

---

# 11. Employee Responsibilities

Employees are expected to:

- Follow workplace policies
- Communicate concerns appropriately
- Cooperate with relevant workplace processes
- Provide accurate information
- Treat colleagues professionally
- Participate in agreed resolution processes

---

# 12. Conflict Resolution

Where appropriate, workplace conflicts may be addressed through:

- Informal discussion
- Manager intervention
- HRBP facilitation
- Mediation
- Structured meetings
- Clarification of responsibilities
- Communication agreements
- Team interventions

The appropriate method depends on the nature and seriousness of the matter.

---

# 13. Grievance Management

A grievance process should generally include:

1. Complaint received
2. Complaint acknowledged
3. Initial assessment
4. Relevant information gathered
5. Investigation where appropriate
6. Employee response considered
7. Findings documented
8. Appropriate decision or recommendation
9. Outcome communicated
10. Follow-up conducted

Organizations should adapt the process to their policies and applicable legal requirements.

---

# 14. Disciplinary Matters

Where misconduct is established through an appropriate process, the organization may consider proportionate action in accordance with its policies and applicable law.

Potential actions may include:

- Coaching
- Counselling
- Written warning
- Final warning
- Other disciplinary measures permitted by organizational policy and applicable law

The appropriate action depends on the facts, severity, history and applicable framework.

---

# 15. Performance Management vs Misconduct

The HRBP should distinguish between:

### Performance

The employee is unable or failing to meet required performance expectations.

### Misconduct

The employee is alleged to have engaged in conduct that breaches an applicable workplace rule or standard.

The distinction matters because the appropriate management process may differ.

---

# 16. Documentation

Employee-relations documentation should be:

- Accurate
- Objective
- Relevant
- Timely
- Confidential
- Factual
- Consistent

Avoid unnecessary personal opinions or unsupported conclusions.

---

# 17. Employee Relations Case Record

A case record may contain:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Case ID | Unique case reference |
| Date Opened | Date case began |
| Department | Relevant department |
| Case Type | Grievance / conduct / performance / etc. |
| Summary | Concise description |
| People Involved | Relevant parties |
| Risk Level | Low / Medium / High / Critical |
| Investigation Required | Yes / No |
| Case Owner | HR / HRBP |
| Action Taken | Intervention |
| Status | Open / Monitoring / Closed |
| Date Closed | Closure date |
| Follow-Up | Required follow-up |

---

# 18. Employee Relations Metrics

The HRBP can monitor:

### Case Volume

Number of employee-relations cases during a defined period.

### Case Resolution Time

Average time taken to resolve cases.

### Repeat Case Rate

Percentage of cases involving recurring issues.

### Grievance Rate

Number of grievances relative to workforce size.

### Absence Trends

Patterns that may indicate workplace-relations issues.

### Turnover Trends

Turnover patterns that may indicate employee-experience concerns.

---

# 19. Root Cause Analysis

Closing a case does not necessarily resolve the underlying organizational problem.

The HRBP should ask:

**Why did this issue occur?**

Possible root causes include:

- Poor communication
- Unclear expectations
- Inadequate management capability
- Workload
- Policy ambiguity
- Poor role design
- Skills gaps
- Team conflict
- Leadership behaviour
- Organizational change

---

# 20. Preventative HRBP Actions

Where recurring issues are identified, the HRBP may recommend:

- Manager training
- Policy clarification
- Leadership coaching
- Communication programmes
- Team interventions
- Role clarification
- Workload review
- Employee listening
- Process improvement

---

# 21. Employee Relations Dashboard

A management dashboard can track:

- Total cases
- Open cases
- Closed cases
- Cases by category
- Cases by department
- Cases by severity
- Average resolution time
- Repeat cases
- Grievance volume
- Conduct cases
- Performance cases
- Absence trends
- Turnover trends

---

# 22. Confidentiality

Employee-relations matters may contain sensitive employee information.

Access should therefore be restricted to authorized personnel.

Information should be shared only where there is a legitimate business, legal or procedural reason to do so.

---

# 23. HRBP Review Questions

During an employee-relations review, ask:

1. What is the actual issue?
2. What evidence exists?
3. Which policy applies?
4. Has the employee been heard?
5. Has the process been consistent?
6. What organizational risk exists?
7. What employee impact exists?
8. What is the root cause?
9. What intervention is appropriate?
10. How can recurrence be prevented?

---

# 24. Business Partnership

Employee relations should not be treated solely as a reactive HR function.

The HRBP should use employee-relations data to identify broader organizational patterns.

For example:

**Repeated grievances → Management capability issue**

**Repeated attendance issues → Work design or engagement issue**

**Repeated turnover → Retention or employee-experience issue**

**Repeated conflicts → Team or leadership issue**

**Repeated policy disputes → Policy clarity issue**

This converts individual cases into organizational insight.

---

# Conclusion

Effective employee relations requires a balance between:

**Fairness + Evidence + Policy + Employee Experience + Business Requirements + Risk Management**

The HRBP's role is to help the organization resolve individual issues appropriately while also identifying the systemic factors that may be creating recurring problems.
