# Disciplinary Process

## Overview

A disciplinary process provides a structured approach for addressing alleged employee misconduct or breaches of workplace standards.

The HRBP's role is to help ensure that disciplinary matters are handled consistently, objectively, proportionately and in accordance with organizational policy and applicable requirements.

---

# 1. Objectives

The disciplinary framework is designed to:

- Establish a consistent process for misconduct matters
- Protect organizational standards
- Give employees an appropriate opportunity to respond
- Ensure decisions are evidence-based
- Promote procedural fairness
- Reduce inconsistent management decisions
- Maintain appropriate documentation
- Support corrective action
- Identify recurring organizational issues

---

# 2. Core Disciplinary Process

The recommended process is:

**Identify → Assess → Investigate → Notify → Hear → Decide → Communicate → Monitor**

The exact procedure should be adapted to the organization's policies and applicable employment requirements.

---

# 3. Performance vs Misconduct

The HRBP should first determine whether the issue is primarily:

### Performance

The employee is not meeting established performance expectations.

Examples:

- Missed targets
- Poor quality of work
- Failure to meet agreed objectives
- Capability gaps

### Misconduct

The employee is alleged to have breached an applicable workplace rule, policy or expected standard of conduct.

Examples:

- Unauthorized absence
- Insubordination
- Misuse of organizational property
- Breach of confidentiality
- Dishonesty
- Unprofessional conduct

The appropriate process depends on the facts and applicable organizational framework.

---

# 4. Step 1 — Identify the Concern

The manager or HRBP should document:

- What allegedly occurred
- Date and location
- People involved
- Relevant policy or rule
- Available evidence
- Immediate business impact
- Any immediate risk

The initial record should distinguish between:

**Allegation vs Established Fact**

---

# 5. Step 2 — Initial HRBP Assessment

The HRBP should assess:

1. Is there a potential policy breach?
2. Is the matter performance-related or conduct-related?
3. Is investigation required?
4. Is the issue sufficiently serious to require escalation?
5. Are there immediate workplace risks?
6. Is there relevant historical documentation?
7. Has the organization handled similar matters consistently?

---

# 6. Step 3 — Investigation

Where appropriate, an investigation should establish the relevant facts.

Potential evidence includes:

- Documents
- Emails
- Messages
- Attendance records
- CCTV or access records where lawfully available
- Employee statements
- Manager statements
- Witness accounts
- Relevant policies
- Other appropriate records

The investigator should remain objective and avoid assuming guilt before the process is completed.

---

# 7. Employee Response

Where a disciplinary outcome may be considered, the employee should ordinarily receive an appropriate opportunity to respond to the concerns or allegations, subject to applicable policy and law.

The employee's response should be documented.

---

# 8. Notice to Employee

Where required by the organization's process, the employee may receive written notice setting out:

- The concern or allegation
- Relevant policy or standard
- Relevant facts available at that stage
- Required response
- Response deadline
- Meeting details, where applicable

The notice should be factual and avoid presenting unproven allegations as established facts.

---

# 9. Disciplinary Meeting

A disciplinary meeting may provide an opportunity to:

- Explain the concern
- Present relevant evidence
- Hear the employee's response
- Clarify disputed facts
- Ask relevant questions
- Consider mitigating information

The meeting should be documented appropriately.

---

# 10. Evidence Assessment

Evidence should be assessed based on:

- Relevance
- Reliability
- Consistency
- Corroboration
- Context
- Timing

The decision-maker should distinguish:

**Allegation → Evidence → Finding → Action**

---

# 11. Employee Explanation

The employee's explanation should be considered before a final decision is made.

Relevant considerations may include:

- Whether the employee accepts or disputes the allegation
- Supporting evidence
- Context
- Intent where relevant
- Previous instructions
- Training received
- Mitigating circumstances
- Previous disciplinary history where legitimately relevant

---

# 12. Findings

The organization should use its established terminology for disciplinary findings.

Possible categories include:

### Substantiated

Available evidence supports the allegation.

### Partially Substantiated

Some elements are supported while others are not.

### Unsubstantiated

Available evidence does not sufficiently support the allegation.

### Inconclusive

Available evidence is insufficient to reach a reliable conclusion.

---

# 13. Decision-Making Factors

Where a disciplinary response is appropriate, management may consider:

- Seriousness of the conduct
- Actual or potential impact
- Relevant organizational policy
- Employee explanation
- Previous relevant history
- Consistency with comparable cases
- Mitigating factors
- Aggravating factors
- Applicable employment requirements

---

# 14. Proportionality

Any disciplinary response should be proportionate to the circumstances and consistent with applicable organizational policies and requirements.

The HRBP should help management avoid:

- Excessive responses
- Inconsistent responses
- Unsupported assumptions
- Decisions based on personal relationships
- Decisions based solely on emotion

---

# 15. Corrective and Disciplinary Actions

Depending on organizational policy and applicable requirements, actions may include:

- Coaching
- Counselling
- Formal warning
- Final warning
- Performance or conduct improvement measures
- Other proportionate disciplinary action
- Termination where permitted and appropriate

The appropriate response should depend on the facts and governing framework.

---

# 16. Consistency and Precedent

The HRBP should consider how comparable cases have previously been handled.

This does not mean every case must receive exactly the same outcome.

Relevant differences may include:

- Severity
- Evidence
- Employee circumstances
- Previous history
- Role
- Business impact
- Applicable policy

The objective is consistent and defensible decision-making.

---

# 17. Mitigating Factors

Depending on the circumstances, mitigating factors may include:

- Limited experience
- Lack of adequate training
- Genuine misunderstanding
- Personal circumstances
- Cooperation with the investigation
- Immediate corrective action
- Strong previous record

Mitigating factors should be assessed objectively rather than assumed.

---

# 18. Aggravating Factors

Depending on the circumstances, aggravating factors may include:

- Repeated conduct
- Deliberate policy breach
- Significant business impact
- Concealment
- Refusal to follow reasonable instructions
- Abuse of position
- Lack of corrective response

The relevance of each factor depends on the specific case.

---

# 19. Outcome Communication

The employee should receive appropriate communication regarding the outcome.

The communication may include:

- Finding
- Relevant action
- Effective date
- Expected future conduct
- Required improvement
- Review period
- Appeal rights where applicable

Confidential information relating to other employees should not be unnecessarily disclosed.

---

# 20. Appeal / Review

Where an appeal mechanism exists, the employee should be informed of:

- Right to appeal
- Grounds for appeal
- Appeal deadline
- Submission method
- Appropriate appeal decision-maker

Where practicable, the appeal should be reviewed by someone who was not responsible for the original decision.

---

A case tracker may include:

| Case ID | Unique case reference |

---

# 22. Disciplinary Metrics

The HRBP can monitor:

### Case Volume

Total disciplinary cases during a defined period.

### Cases by Department

Identifies departments with higher case volumes.

### Cases by Category

Identifies recurring misconduct patterns.

### Average Resolution Time

Measures how long cases remain open.

### Repeat Misconduct Rate

Measures recurring conduct issues.

### Appeal Rate

Measures the proportion of decisions that are appealed.

### Outcome Distribution

Tracks disciplinary outcomes by category.

---

# 23. Root Cause Analysis

The HRBP should investigate recurring patterns.

For example:

**Repeated attendance cases → Attendance management issue**

**Repeated policy breaches → Policy awareness issue**

**Repeated manager conflicts → Management capability issue**

**Repeated safety incidents → Training or process issue**

**Repeated misconduct in one area → Local leadership or culture issue**

---

# 24. HRBP Role

The HRBP should:

- Advise managers
- Review applicable policy
- Support fair process
- Assess organizational risk
- Facilitate investigations where appropriate
- Review consistency
- Maintain appropriate documentation
- Support decision-making
- Monitor trends
- Recommend preventative interventions

The HRBP should not predetermine the outcome of an investigation.

---

# 25. Manager Role

Managers should:

- Clearly communicate expectations
- Document relevant events
- Escalate appropriate matters
- Cooperate with investigations
- Avoid retaliation
- Apply workplace standards consistently
- Implement agreed corrective actions

---

# 26. Employee Role

Employees should:

- Follow applicable workplace policies
- Participate appropriately in investigations
- Provide accurate information
- Respond to legitimate workplace concerns
- Comply with reasonable workplace expectations

---

# 27. HRBP Decision Checklist

Before recommending disciplinary action, ask:

1. What exactly is alleged?
2. What evidence exists?
3. Which policy applies?
4. Was the employee given an appropriate opportunity to respond?
5. What does the evidence establish?
6. Are there mitigating circumstances?
7. Are there aggravating circumstances?
8. How have comparable cases been handled?
9. Is the proposed action proportionate?
10. Has the decision been appropriately documented?
11. Is an appeal mechanism available?
12. Is there a systemic issue that needs attention?

---

# 28. Example Case

### Allegation

An employee repeatedly failed to follow an established operational procedure.

### Investigation

The HRBP reviews:

- Relevant procedure
- Training records
- Manager instructions
- Previous warnings
- Employee explanation
- Operational impact

### Finding

Evidence establishes repeated non-compliance despite prior communication and training.

### HRBP Recommendation

Management considers an appropriate corrective or disciplinary response consistent with policy and comparable cases.

### Preventative Action

The organization also reviews whether:

- The procedure is clear
- Training is effective
- Managers are monitoring compliance
- Employees understand the consequences of non-compliance

---

# 29. Important Principle

Disciplinary management should not be used simply as punishment.

The HRBP should consider the broader objective:

**Protect Standards + Correct Behaviour + Manage Risk + Maintain Fairness**

Where appropriate, corrective action should help prevent recurrence.

---

# 30. Conclusion

A structured disciplinary process helps organizations manage alleged misconduct consistently and responsibly.

The HRBP connects:

**Policy + Evidence + Employee Response + Consistency + Proportionality + Business Risk**

The objective is to ensure that disciplinary decisions are based on a sound process rather than assumptions, personal relationships or emotional reactions.
