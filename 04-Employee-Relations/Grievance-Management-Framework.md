# Grievance Management Framework

## Overview

A grievance management framework provides a structured process through which employees can raise workplace concerns and through which the organization can assess, investigate and respond to those concerns appropriately.

The HRBP's role is to ensure that grievances are handled consistently, objectively, confidentially and in accordance with organizational policy and applicable requirements.

---

# 1. Objectives

The grievance framework is designed to:

- Provide employees with a clear channel for raising concerns
- Encourage early resolution of workplace issues
- Ensure concerns are appropriately investigated
- Promote consistency and fairness
- Reduce unnecessary escalation
- Identify recurring organizational problems
- Protect employee and organizational interests
- Improve employee experience
- Create appropriate documentation
- Support management decision-making

---

# 2. Core Grievance Process

The recommended process is:

**Receive → Acknowledge → Assess → Investigate → Review → Decide → Communicate → Follow Up**

---

# 3. Step 1 — Receive the Grievance

A grievance may be received through an approved organizational channel such as:

- HR
- HRBP
- Line Manager
- Employee Relations function
- Formal grievance system

The organization should maintain appropriate records of the grievance.

---

# 4. Step 2 — Acknowledge

The grievance should be acknowledged within the organization's established timeframe.

The acknowledgement should confirm:

- Receipt of the grievance
- That the concern will be reviewed
- The next stage of the process
- Any information required from the employee
- The appropriate contact person

Acknowledgement does not mean that the organization has accepted the allegations as established facts.

---

# 5. Step 3 — Initial Assessment

The HRBP should determine:

- What is being alleged?
- Who is involved?
- When did the issue occur?
- Which policies may apply?
- Is the matter urgent?
- Is investigation required?
- Are there immediate workplace risks?
- Is another process more appropriate?

---

# 6. Grievance Classification

Grievances may relate to:

| Category | Example |
|---|---|
| Management | Concern about management treatment |
| Compensation | Pay or benefit concern |
| Working Conditions | Workplace environment |
| Policy | Concern regarding policy application |
| Workload | Excessive or unreasonable workload concern |
| Interpersonal | Conflict with colleague |
| Career | Promotion or development concern |
| Conduct | Alleged inappropriate behaviour |
| Discrimination / Harassment | Allegation requiring appropriate escalation |
| Organizational Change | Concern about restructuring or change |

The organization should adapt categories to its own policies and applicable requirements.

---

# 7. Grievance Severity

### Low

Concern that can potentially be resolved through discussion or manager intervention.

### Moderate

Concern requiring HRBP involvement and structured review.

### High

Concern involving significant employee, organizational or reputational risk.

### Critical

Concern requiring immediate escalation to appropriate senior, legal or specialist support.

Severity should be based on the facts and circumstances of each case.

---

# 8. Immediate Risk Assessment

Before proceeding, the HRBP should consider whether there is an immediate risk involving:

- Employee safety
- Workplace violence
- Serious misconduct
- Retaliation
- Confidential information
- Regulatory exposure
- Significant business disruption

Where immediate risk exists, appropriate escalation and protective measures should be considered.

---

# 9. Investigation

Where investigation is appropriate, the investigator should gather relevant information.

Potential sources include:

- Employee statement
- Manager statement
- Witness accounts
- Emails
- Messages
- Policies
- Attendance records
- Relevant HR records
- Other available evidence

The investigation should focus on establishing facts.

---

# 10. Employee Interview

The employee raising the grievance should have an appropriate opportunity to explain:

- What happened
- Who was involved
- When it occurred
- How it affected them
- What evidence exists
- What resolution they are seeking

Questions should be neutral and fact-focused.

---

# 11. Respondent Interview

Where allegations concern another employee or manager, that individual should ordinarily have an appropriate opportunity to respond, subject to the applicable process and circumstances.

The response should be documented.

---

# 12. Witnesses

Witnesses may be interviewed where their information is relevant.

Witness questions should focus on:

- What they personally observed
- What they personally heard
- When the event occurred
- Who was present
- What evidence they may have

Witnesses should not be encouraged to speculate about matters they did not personally observe.

---

# 13. Evidence Assessment

Evidence should be assessed based on:

- Relevance
- Reliability
- Consistency
- Corroboration
- Timing
- Context

The HRBP should distinguish:

**Allegation ≠ Evidence ≠ Finding**

---

# 14. Grievance Findings

At the conclusion of the review, findings may be categorized according to the organization's established framework.

For example:

### Substantiated

Available evidence supports the concern.

### Partially Substantiated

Some elements are supported while others are not.

### Unsubstantiated

Available evidence does not sufficiently support the concern.

### Inconclusive

Available evidence is insufficient to reach a reliable conclusion.

Organizations should use terminology consistent with their own policies and applicable requirements.

---

# 15. Resolution Options

Depending on the nature of the grievance, resolution may include:

- Clarification
- Manager intervention
- Mediation
- Coaching
- Policy clarification
- Corrective action
- Process improvement
- Training
- Workplace adjustment
- Other appropriate organizational action

The appropriate response should depend on the facts and applicable policies.

---

# 16. Communicating the Outcome

The organization should communicate the outcome appropriately.

The communication may include:

- Confirmation that the review has concluded
- General outcome
- Relevant action taken
- Any agreed next steps
- Available escalation or appeal process, where applicable

Confidential information about other employees should not be unnecessarily disclosed.

---

# 17. Appeal or Review

Where the organization's policy provides an appeal or review mechanism, the employee should be informed of:

- The right to appeal or request review
- Applicable grounds
- Timeframe
- Submission method
- Person or body responsible for reviewing the matter

The appeal process should be handled by an appropriately independent person where practicable.

---

# 18. Non-Retaliation

Employees should be able to raise legitimate workplace concerns without inappropriate retaliation.

The organization should monitor for potential retaliatory behaviour where circumstances warrant.

Examples of potential retaliation concerns may include:

- Threats
- Intimidation
- Unjustified adverse treatment
- Deliberate exclusion
- Punitive conduct unrelated to legitimate business reasons

Any such concern should be assessed separately and appropriately.

---

# 19. Confidentiality

Grievance information should be handled confidentially and shared only with individuals who have a legitimate reason to receive it.

Confidentiality does not necessarily mean absolute secrecy.

The organization may need to share relevant information with appropriate decision-makers, investigators, legal advisers or other authorized persons.

---

---

# 21. Grievance Metrics

The HRBP can monitor:

### Grievance Volume

Number of grievances received during a defined period.

### Grievance Rate

Number of grievances relative to workforce size.

### Resolution Time

Average time taken to resolve grievances.

### Repeat Grievance Rate

Percentage of grievances involving recurring issues.

### Department Distribution

Grievances by department.

### Category Distribution

Grievances by issue type.

---

# 22. Grievance Rate

### Formula

**Number of Grievances ÷ Average Headcount × 100**

Example:

If an organization records 14 grievances with an average workforce of 700:

**14 ÷ 700 × 100 = 2%**

---

# 23. Root Cause Analysis

The HRBP should look beyond individual grievances.

For recurring concerns, investigate potential systemic causes such as:

- Management capability
- Communication failures
- Policy ambiguity
- Workload
- Compensation structures
- Organizational change
- Poor role design
- Team dynamics
- Leadership behaviour
- Employee expectations

---

# 24. From Grievance to Organizational Insight

The HRBP can identify patterns such as:

**Repeated workload complaints → Workforce planning issue**

**Repeated manager complaints → Leadership capability issue**

**Repeated compensation grievances → Reward communication issue**

**Repeated policy disputes → Policy clarity issue**

**Repeated interpersonal conflict → Team or management issue**

This transforms grievance management from a reactive function into a strategic HR intelligence source.

---

# 25. HRBP Responsibilities

The HRBP should:

- Provide process guidance
- Assess risk
- Facilitate appropriate investigations
- Support managers
- Maintain appropriate documentation
- Promote consistency
- Monitor recurring themes
- Recommend organizational interventions
- Escalate serious matters appropriately
- Protect confidentiality

---

# 26. Manager Responsibilities

Managers should:

- Listen to employee concerns
- Avoid dismissing complaints prematurely
- Escalate appropriate matters to HR
- Preserve relevant information
- Avoid retaliation
- Cooperate with investigations
- Implement agreed actions

---

# 27. Employee Responsibilities

Employees should:

- Raise concerns through appropriate channels
- Provide accurate information
- Cooperate with relevant reviews
- Respect confidentiality
- Participate constructively in resolution efforts

---

# 28. HRBP Review Questions

Before closing a grievance, ask:

1. What was the original concern?
2. What evidence was reviewed?
3. Was the employee appropriately heard?
4. Were relevant parties given an opportunity to respond?
5. What does the evidence establish?
6. Which policy applies?
7. What action is appropriate?
8. Has the root cause been identified?
9. Is follow-up required?
10. Could this issue recur?
11. Is there a wider organizational issue?
12. What management action is required?

---

# 29. Example Case

### Concern

An employee reports that workload expectations have increased significantly without corresponding role clarification.

### Initial Assessment

The HRBP reviews:

- Job description
- Current workload
- Team structure
- Manager expectations
- Work allocation
- Previous communications

### Finding

The concern is partly attributable to an organizational restructuring that changed responsibilities without sufficiently clear communication.

### HRBP Action

1. Clarify responsibilities.
2. Review workload allocation.
3. Update role expectations.
4. Support manager communication.
5. Monitor workload over the following quarter.

### Organizational Insight

The grievance indicates a broader change-management and role-clarity issue.

---

# 30. Conclusion

A strong grievance-management process provides employees with a structured mechanism for raising concerns while giving the organization a reliable framework for investigation, resolution and organizational learning.

The HRBP should connect:

**Employee Voice + Evidence + Fair Process + Business Risk + Organizational Learning**

The goal is not simply to close cases.

The goal is to resolve legitimate concerns, reduce recurrence and strengthen the organization.
