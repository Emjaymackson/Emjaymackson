# Conflict Resolution Framework

## Overview

Conflict resolution is the structured process of identifying, addressing and resolving workplace disagreements in a fair, constructive and business-focused manner.

The HRBP helps managers and employees move from conflict toward workable solutions while identifying any underlying organizational issues.

---

## 1. Objectives

The framework aims to:

- Resolve workplace conflict early
- Reduce unnecessary escalation
- Protect working relationships
- Improve communication
- Support managers
- Promote constructive employee relations
- Identify root causes
- Prevent recurring conflict
- Protect organizational effectiveness

---

## 2. Conflict Resolution Process

The core process is:

**Identify → Understand → Assess → Engage → Resolve → Document → Follow Up**

---

## 3. Types of Workplace Conflict

Common examples include:

- Employee-to-employee conflict
- Manager-employee conflict
- Team conflict
- Communication breakdown
- Role ambiguity
- Workload disputes
- Resource allocation disputes
- Performance disagreements
- Personality-related conflict
- Policy-related disagreements
- Organizational change-related conflict

---

## 4. Initial Conflict Assessment

The HRBP should establish:

1. What is the disagreement?
2. Who is involved?
3. What caused the conflict?
4. How long has it existed?
5. Has the issue escalated?
6. What impact is it having?
7. What attempts have already been made to resolve it?
8. Is there a policy issue?
9. Is formal investigation required?
10. What resolution would be realistic?

---

## 5. Conflict Severity

### Level 1 — Minor

A disagreement that can reasonably be addressed through direct communication.

### Level 2 — Moderate

A conflict affecting working relationships or productivity and requiring management or HR involvement.

### Level 3 — Serious

A conflict involving significant operational, employee-relations or organizational risk.

### Level 4 — Critical

A matter involving serious allegations, safety concerns, potential legal exposure or significant business disruption requiring appropriate escalation.

---

## 6. Informal Resolution

Where appropriate, the first response may involve:

- Direct conversation
- Manager intervention
- Clarification of expectations
- Role clarification
- Coaching
- Facilitated discussion
- Team discussion

Informal resolution should not be used where the nature of the allegation or risk requires a formal process.

---

## 7. HRBP-Facilitated Resolution

The HRBP may facilitate a structured discussion between the parties.

The discussion should focus on:

- Facts
- Impact
- Expectations
- Interests
- Future behaviour
- Practical solutions

The HRBP should avoid turning the discussion into an opportunity for personal attacks.

---

## 8. Conflict Resolution Meeting

A structured meeting may follow this format:

### Opening

Explain the purpose of the meeting.

### Employee A Perspective

Allow the employee to explain their concerns.

### Employee B Perspective

Allow the other employee to explain their perspective.

### Clarification

Identify areas of agreement and disagreement.

### Impact

Discuss the effect of the conflict on work.

### Resolution

Identify practical solutions.

### Agreement

Confirm actions and responsibilities.

### Follow-Up

Set a review date.

---

## 9. Communication Principles

Participants should be encouraged to:

- Speak respectfully
- Focus on behaviour rather than personality
- Use specific examples
- Listen actively
- Avoid interruptions
- Avoid personal attacks
- Focus on solutions
- Respect confidentiality

---

## 10. Root Cause Analysis

The HRBP should determine whether the conflict is caused by:

- Poor communication
- Role ambiguity
- Leadership style
- Workload
- Resource constraints
- Competing priorities
- Performance issues
- Personality differences
- Organizational change
- Policy ambiguity
- Poor management practices

Resolving the immediate disagreement without addressing the root cause may allow the conflict to return.

---

## 11. Resolution Options

Depending on the circumstances, possible interventions include:

- Clarifying responsibilities
- Adjusting workflows
- Setting communication expectations
- Coaching
- Mediation
- Team intervention
- Manager coaching
- Role clarification
- Workload review
- Policy clarification
- Formal investigation where necessary

---

## 12. Mediation

Mediation may be appropriate where parties are willing to participate and the matter is suitable for facilitated resolution.

The mediator should:

- Remain neutral
- Establish ground rules
- Allow each party to speak
- Identify areas of disagreement
- Identify common interests
- Facilitate practical solutions
- Document agreed actions where appropriate

Mediation should not replace a required formal investigation where serious allegations require investigation.

---

## 13. Conflict Resolution Agreement

Where appropriate, the parties may agree to:

- Communicate respectfully
- Follow agreed reporting lines
- Clarify responsibilities
- Avoid specific behaviours
- Use agreed communication channels
- Raise future concerns appropriately
- Review progress after an agreed period

---

## 14. Follow-Up

Conflict resolution should not end immediately after the meeting.

The HRBP or manager should assess:

- Has the relationship improved?
- Are agreed actions being followed?
- Has the conflict reappeared?
- Has productivity improved?
- Are additional interventions required?

---

## 15. Conflict Case Tracker

A conflict tracker may contain:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Case ID | Unique reference |
| Date Opened | Date conflict was reported |
| Department | Relevant department |
| Parties | Employees involved |
| Conflict Type | Nature of disagreement |
| Severity | Risk level |
| Root Cause | Identified cause |
| Intervention | Action taken |
| Owner | HR / Manager |
| Status | Open / Monitoring / Closed |
| Review Date | Follow-up date |
| Outcome | Resolution status |

---

## 16. Conflict Metrics

The HRBP can monitor:

### Conflict Case Volume

Number of workplace conflict cases during a defined period.

### Resolution Time

Average time taken to resolve conflict cases.

### Repeat Conflict Rate

Percentage of cases involving recurring conflict.

### Department Distribution

Conflict cases by department.

### Resolution Method

Percentage resolved through:

- Manager intervention
- HR facilitation
- Mediation
- Formal process
- Other interventions

---

## 17. Example Case

### Situation

Two department supervisors repeatedly disagree about responsibility for shared resources.

### Initial Assessment

The HRBP identifies that the conflict is partly caused by unclear responsibilities.

### Intervention

The HRBP facilitates a meeting and helps both managers clarify:

- Responsibilities
- Approval authority
- Communication channels
- Escalation process

### Outcome

A written responsibility framework is agreed and reviewed after 60 days.

### Organizational Learning

The conflict reveals a role-design problem rather than simply an interpersonal disagreement.

---

## 18. HRBP Role

The HRBP should:

- Diagnose the nature of the conflict
- Assess risk
- Determine appropriate intervention
- Facilitate resolution where appropriate
- Coach managers
- Monitor recurring patterns
- Identify systemic causes
- Recommend preventative action

---

## 19. Manager Role

Managers should:

- Address conflict early
- Communicate expectations
- Listen to employees
- Avoid taking sides without understanding the facts
- Escalate serious matters
- Support agreed solutions
- Monitor working relationships

---

## 20. Employee Role

Employees should:

- Raise concerns appropriately
- Communicate respectfully
- Participate constructively
- Focus on work-related issues
- Follow agreed resolution actions
- Avoid retaliation

---

## 21. HRBP Review Questions

Before closing a conflict case, ask:

1. What caused the conflict?
2. Has each party been heard?
3. Is there a policy issue?
4. Is formal investigation required?
5. What resolution has been agreed?
6. Who owns each action?
7. When will progress be reviewed?
8. Has the root cause been addressed?
9. Could the conflict recur?
10. Is there a wider organizational issue?

---

## 22. Conclusion

Effective conflict resolution moves the organization from:

**Disagreement → Understanding → Resolution → Improved Working Relationship**

The HRBP adds value by addressing both the immediate conflict and the organizational conditions that created it.

The goal is not simply to make a disagreement disappear.

The goal is to establish a sustainable working environment in which employees can perform effectively and workplace issues can be addressed constructively.
