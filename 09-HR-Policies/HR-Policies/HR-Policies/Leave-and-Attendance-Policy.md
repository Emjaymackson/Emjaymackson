# Leave and Attendance Policy

## 1. Purpose

The Leave and Attendance Policy establishes clear expectations regarding employee attendance, working hours, leave requests, absence reporting, and time-off management.

The policy is designed to support:

* Consistent attendance management
* Business continuity
* Employee wellbeing
* Fair and transparent leave administration
* Accurate HR records
* Manager accountability
* Consistent application of workplace requirements

This is a **fictionalized portfolio policy** and should be reviewed and adapted to the organization's employment contracts, applicable laws, collective agreements where applicable, and professional legal advice before implementation.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to employees of the organization, subject to the terms of their employment contracts and any applicable organizational arrangements.

Different employment categories may have specific attendance or leave provisions.

Where a specific contractual or legally applicable requirement differs from this general policy, the appropriate requirement should be followed.

---

# 3. Policy Principles

The organization will manage attendance and leave based on the following principles:

### Consistency

Similar situations should be handled consistently.

### Transparency

Employees should understand attendance and leave expectations.

### Planning

Employees should provide reasonable notice for planned leave.

### Accountability

Employees and managers share responsibility for effective attendance management.

### Business Continuity

Leave should be planned in a way that supports operational requirements.

### Employee Wellbeing

Legitimate health, family, and personal circumstances should be handled appropriately and respectfully.

### Confidentiality

Personal information associated with leave and absence should be handled appropriately.

---

# 4. Working Hours

Employees are expected to work the hours specified in their employment contracts, departmental schedules, or approved work arrangements.

Working hours may vary according to:

* Job role
* Department
* Business requirements
* Shift arrangements
* Remote or hybrid work arrangements
* Customer requirements

Employees should be available and ready to perform their duties during their approved working hours.

---

# 5. Attendance Expectations

Employees are expected to:

* Report to work on time
* Remain available during scheduled working hours
* Follow attendance recording procedures
* Notify their manager when they will be late or absent
* Obtain appropriate approval for planned absence
* Follow departmental scheduling requirements
* Communicate changes that may affect attendance
* Return to work as scheduled following approved leave

Repeated or unexplained attendance issues may require management review.

---

# 6. Punctuality

Employees should report to work at the scheduled start time.

Repeated lateness may affect:

* Team operations
* Customer service
* Productivity
* Work allocation
* Colleague workload

Managers should address recurring lateness promptly and consistently.

Before taking formal action, the manager should establish the relevant facts and provide the employee with an opportunity to explain the circumstances.

---

# 7. Attendance Recording

Where an attendance recording system is used, employees may be required to record:

* Start time
* End time
* Breaks where applicable
* Shift attendance
* Approved absence
* Remote work days where applicable

Employees must not falsify attendance records or record attendance on behalf of another employee.

---

# 8. Leave Categories

The organization may provide different categories of leave based on applicable requirements and organizational policy.

Examples include:

* Annual leave
* Sick leave
* Maternity leave
* Paternity leave
* Compassionate leave
* Family-related leave
* Study leave
* Official duty leave
* Unpaid leave
* Other approved leave categories

The organization's specific leave entitlements should be stated clearly in the applicable employment documentation and policy.

---

# 9. Annual Leave

Annual leave provides employees with approved time away from work for rest and personal purposes.

Employees should:

* Submit leave requests through the approved process
* Provide reasonable notice
* Obtain approval before commencing planned leave
* Ensure appropriate work handover where required
* Return to work on the agreed date

Managers should consider operational requirements while applying the organization's leave provisions consistently.

---

# 10. Leave Request Process

The standard process should be:

```text id="q4j3v2"
Employee Identifies Need for Leave
        ↓
Employee Submits Leave Request
        ↓
Manager Reviews Request
        ↓
Operational Impact Considered
        ↓
Approval / Decline / Alternative Date
        ↓
HR Record Updated
        ↓
Employee Takes Approved Leave
        ↓
Employee Returns to Work
```

Leave should not normally be treated as approved until the appropriate authorization has been provided.

---

# 11. Leave Request Information

A leave request may contain:

* Employee name
* Department
* Leave type
* Start date
* End date
* Number of working days
* Reason where required by the applicable leave process
* Handover arrangements
* Manager approval
* Supporting documentation where required

Sensitive personal information should not be collected unnecessarily.

---

# 12. Manager Responsibilities

Managers are responsible for:

* Reviewing leave requests promptly
* Considering operational requirements
* Applying leave rules consistently
* Monitoring team coverage
* Confirming approvals
* Maintaining appropriate confidentiality
* Addressing recurring attendance concerns
* Escalating unusual or serious issues to HR

Managers should not discourage employees from taking legitimate leave or create informal leave requirements outside approved organizational processes.

---

# 13. HR Responsibilities

HR is responsible for:

* Maintaining leave records
* Supporting managers
* Advising employees
* Monitoring leave balances where applicable
* Maintaining attendance records
* Identifying recurring attendance trends
* Supporting consistent policy application
* Reviewing attendance-related employee relations issues
* Updating policy requirements when necessary

---

# 14. Sick Leave and Illness

Employees who are unable to work due to illness should notify their manager as soon as reasonably possible and follow the organization's absence reporting procedure.

Where documentation is required, the employee should provide the appropriate documentation through the approved HR process.

Managers should avoid requesting unnecessary medical details.

Information relating to an employee's health should be treated confidentially and handled in accordance with applicable privacy requirements.

---

# 15. Unplanned Absence

Where an employee cannot attend work unexpectedly, the employee should:

1. Notify the manager or designated contact.
2. Explain that they will be absent.
3. Provide an expected return date where reasonably possible.
4. Follow any required documentation process.
5. Maintain reasonable communication where the absence continues.

Failure to follow the absence reporting process may result in the matter being reviewed by the manager and HR.

---

# 16. Emergency Absence

The organization recognizes that emergencies may occur.

Examples include:

* Serious family emergencies
* Unexpected accidents
* Urgent personal circumstances
* Other unforeseen events

Employees should notify their manager as soon as reasonably practicable.

Managers should consider the circumstances and seek HR guidance where necessary.

---

# 17. Unauthorised Absence

An absence may be treated as unauthorized where an employee:

* Fails to report to work without appropriate notification
* Fails to obtain required approval
* Does not return after approved leave without explanation
* Repeatedly fails to follow the absence reporting process

Unauthorized absence should be investigated before formal action is taken.

The employee should be given an appropriate opportunity to explain the circumstances.

---

# 18. Extended Absence

Where an employee is absent for an extended period, HR and the manager should maintain appropriate communication.

Depending on the circumstances, the organization may:

* Review expected return dates
* Assess operational requirements
* Discuss available support
* Review applicable leave provisions
* Consider temporary work arrangements where appropriate
* Maintain accurate HR records

Any action should be consistent with applicable requirements and organizational policy.

---

# 19. Return to Work

Following certain periods of absence, the employee may be required to participate in a return-to-work discussion.

The purpose may be to:

* Confirm the employee has returned
* Understand any workplace support needs
* Identify recurring attendance issues
* Confirm outstanding work
* Discuss reasonable operational adjustments where applicable

The discussion should focus on workplace needs and employee support rather than unnecessary personal information.

---

# 20. Public Holidays

Employees will observe public holidays recognized as applicable to their employment and work location, subject to organizational requirements.

Where business operations require employees to work on a public holiday, the organization should apply the relevant contractual, statutory, or organizational arrangements.

---

# 21. Remote and Hybrid Attendance

Where remote or hybrid work is approved, employees remain subject to attendance and availability expectations.

Employees should:

* Be available during agreed working hours
* Attend required meetings
* Maintain communication
* Complete assigned work
* Protect company information
* Follow approved remote-work arrangements
* Notify managers of inability to work

Remote work should not be treated as an absence from work.

---

# 22. Flexible Working Arrangements

Where flexible working arrangements are available, they should be:

* Formally approved
* Clearly documented
* Consistent with business requirements
* Applied fairly
* Reviewed periodically where necessary

Examples may include:

* Adjusted start and finish times
* Hybrid work
* Approved remote work
* Shift arrangements
* Other approved flexibility

---

# 23. Leave Handover

Before commencing planned leave, employees should provide an appropriate handover where required.

A handover may include:

* Current tasks
* Pending deadlines
* Client matters
* Key contacts
* Documents
* Outstanding approvals
* Urgent issues
* Temporary responsibility assignments

The level of handover required should depend on the employee's role.

---

# 24. Leave During Critical Business Periods

Certain periods may have significant operational requirements.

Examples may include:

* Major projects
* Audits
* Peak customer periods
* Product launches
* Financial reporting periods
* Critical operational activities

Managers may need to coordinate leave carefully during such periods while still applying approved leave provisions fairly and appropriately.

---

# 25. Leave Records

HR should maintain accurate records of:

* Leave requests
* Approved leave
* Leave taken
* Leave balances where applicable
* Absence records
* Supporting documentation where required

Access to sensitive employee information should be limited to authorized personnel.

---

# 26. Attendance Monitoring

Attendance monitoring should focus on identifying patterns rather than unnecessarily tracking individual employees.

Potential indicators include:

* Repeated lateness
* Frequent unplanned absence
* Patterns around particular days
* Excessive unauthorized absence
* Repeated failure to follow reporting procedures
* Significant changes in attendance

A pattern should be reviewed in context before conclusions are reached.

---

# 27. Attendance Review Process

Where attendance concerns arise, the manager should generally follow this process:

```text id="p9j1as"
Identify Concern
        ↓
Review Attendance Records
        ↓
Establish Facts
        ↓
Discuss With Employee
        ↓
Understand Circumstances
        ↓
Determine Appropriate Support / Action
        ↓
Monitor Progress
        ↓
Escalate to HR Where Necessary
```

---

# 28. Attendance and Employee Relations

Attendance issues can sometimes involve underlying workplace concerns.

The HRBP should consider whether attendance patterns may be associated with:

* Workload
* Manager relationship
* Workplace conflict
* Employee wellbeing
* Job dissatisfaction
* Scheduling issues
* Work environment
* Lack of role clarity

These factors should be investigated appropriately rather than assumed.

---

# 29. Absence and Performance

Attendance and performance are related but should not automatically be treated as the same issue.

An employee may have legitimate absences while still performing effectively.

Similarly, an employee may have good attendance while experiencing performance challenges.

Managers should therefore assess:

* Attendance
* Performance
* Conduct
* Context

as distinct but potentially related areas.

# 32. Attendance Metrics

### Attendance Rate

```text id="1g7wq6"
Attendance Rate =
Scheduled Working Days - Absence Days
÷ Scheduled Working Days × 100
```

### Absence Rate

```text id="5q8c7h"
Absence Rate =
Total Absence Days
÷ Total Scheduled Working Days × 100
```

### Leave Utilization

Where applicable:

```text id="9b4x1c"
Leave Utilization =
Leave Days Taken
÷ Available Leave Days × 100
```

The organization should define the precise methodology used for each metric and apply it consistently.
