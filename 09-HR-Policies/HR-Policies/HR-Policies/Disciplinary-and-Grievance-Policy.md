# Disciplinary and Grievance Policy

## 1. Purpose

The Disciplinary and Grievance Policy establishes a structured approach for addressing workplace misconduct, performance-related concerns where appropriate, employee complaints, and disputes.

The policy is designed to promote:

* Fair and consistent treatment
* Procedural clarity
* Employee accountability
* Manager accountability
* Early resolution of workplace concerns
* Appropriate documentation
* Respectful employee relations
* Organizational risk management

This is a **fictionalized portfolio policy**. It should be reviewed and adapted to the organization's employment contracts, applicable laws, regulatory requirements, collective agreements where applicable, and professional legal advice before implementation.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to employees of the organization, subject to their employment contracts and applicable organizational requirements.

Specific procedures may vary depending on:

* Employment status
* Nature of the concern
* Seniority
* Applicable workplace requirements
* Collective arrangements where applicable
* Legal or regulatory requirements

---

# 3. Policy Principles

Disciplinary and grievance matters should be managed according to the following principles.

### Fairness

Employees should have a reasonable opportunity to understand and respond to concerns raised about them.

### Consistency

Comparable situations should be handled consistently while recognizing that circumstances may differ.

### Objectivity

Decisions should be based on relevant evidence rather than assumptions.

### Confidentiality

Information should be shared only with people who have a legitimate need to know.

### Timeliness

Concerns should be addressed within a reasonable timeframe.

### Documentation

Material decisions and relevant discussions should be appropriately documented.

### Proportionality

Any corrective or disciplinary response should be appropriate to the circumstances and applicable requirements.

### Non-Retaliation

Employees should not be subjected to retaliation for raising legitimate workplace concerns or participating appropriately in an authorized process.

---

# 4. Key Definitions

## Disciplinary Matter

A disciplinary matter concerns an alleged breach of workplace standards, policy, employment obligations, or expected conduct.

## Grievance

A grievance is a formal or informal workplace complaint raised by an employee concerning a workplace issue.

## Misconduct

Misconduct generally refers to behaviour that breaches established workplace standards or obligations.

## Gross or Serious Misconduct

Some forms of misconduct may be sufficiently serious to require a more significant response. The organization should define relevant examples in accordance with applicable requirements.

## Investigation

An investigation is a structured process for establishing relevant facts concerning a workplace allegation or concern.

## Corrective Action

Corrective action refers to steps taken to address a workplace issue and improve future conduct or performance.

# 6. Distinguishing Performance and Conduct

Performance and conduct concerns should not automatically be treated as the same issue.

### Performance Concern

An employee may understand the required standard but struggle to achieve it.

Examples:

* Consistently missing agreed objectives
* Inadequate work quality
* Failure to meet reasonable productivity expectations
* Skills gaps

The appropriate response may involve:

* Coaching
* Training
* Performance feedback
* Clear objectives
* Performance improvement planning

### Conduct Concern

An employee may deliberately or negligently breach an established workplace requirement.

Examples:

* Falsifying records
* Unauthorized disclosure of confidential information
* Harassment
* Deliberate refusal to follow a legitimate instruction

The appropriate response may involve the disciplinary process.

Some situations may involve both performance and conduct considerations.

# 7. Informal Resolution

Not every workplace concern requires a formal process.

Where appropriate, an issue may initially be addressed through:

* Coaching
* Informal discussion
* Clarification of expectations
* Mediation
* Manager intervention
* HR advisory support

Informal resolution should not be used where the seriousness of the matter requires a formal process.

# 9. Initial Assessment

When a concern is reported, HR or management should assess:

* What happened?
* When did it happen?
* Who is involved?
* What policy or expectation may be relevant?
* Is there an immediate risk?
* Is urgent action required?
* Does the matter require investigation?
* Should HR be involved?
* Is specialist advice required?

The initial assessment should not assume that an allegation is proven.

---

# 10. Fact-Finding

Fact-finding may involve:

* Reviewing relevant documents
* Speaking with the employee
* Speaking with witnesses
* Reviewing system records
* Reviewing relevant correspondence
* Examining applicable policies
* Reviewing previous relevant documentation

The objective is to establish the relevant facts.

---

# 11. Employee Opportunity to Respond

Where a formal disciplinary process is being considered, the employee should generally be given an appropriate opportunity to respond to the concerns.

The communication should explain, where appropriate:

* Nature of the concern
* Relevant facts or allegations
* Applicable policy or requirement
* Meeting date
* Employee's opportunity to provide an explanation
* Any applicable representation or support rights

The exact procedure should follow the organization's approved process.

---

# 12. Investigation

An investigation should be proportionate to the seriousness and complexity of the matter.

The investigator may:

* Define the investigation scope
* Identify relevant witnesses
* Gather documents
* Conduct interviews
* Review records
* Compare evidence
* Document findings

The investigator should remain objective and avoid reaching conclusions before reviewing the relevant evidence.

---

# 13. Witnesses

Witnesses may be interviewed where their information is relevant.

Witnesses should be encouraged to:

* Provide factual information
* Avoid speculation
* Maintain appropriate confidentiality
* Cooperate with the investigation

Witness information should be documented appropriately.

---

# 14. Evidence

Potential evidence may include:

* Emails
* Messages
* Attendance records
* HR records
* Performance documentation
* Policies
* Meeting records
* System records
* Relevant documents
* Witness accounts

Evidence should be handled securely and accessed only by authorized individuals.

---

# 15. Investigation Findings

Investigation findings should clearly distinguish between:

* Confirmed facts
* Information provided by individuals
* Allegations
* Conflicting accounts
* Evidence that could not be verified

The investigator should avoid presenting assumptions as established facts.

---

# 16. Disciplinary Meeting

Where a formal disciplinary meeting is required, the employee should have an appropriate opportunity to:

* Respond to the concerns
* Provide relevant evidence
* Explain circumstances
* Clarify disputed information
* Ask relevant questions where appropriate

The decision-maker should consider the information presented before reaching a conclusion.

---

# 17. Decision-Making

A disciplinary decision should consider:

* Established facts
* Relevant policies
* Employee response
* Available evidence
* Seriousness of the conduct
* Relevant circumstances
* Previous relevant disciplinary history where appropriate
* Consistency with organizational practice
* Applicable requirements

Decisions should be documented appropriately.

---

# 18. Disciplinary Outcomes

Depending on the circumstances and applicable requirements, potential outcomes may include:

* No formal action
* Informal counselling
* Coaching
* Formal warning
* Final warning
* Performance improvement measures where appropriate
* Other corrective action
* Termination of employment where justified and lawfully permitted

The organization should define its disciplinary structure clearly.

---

# 19. Warnings

Where warnings are used, the documentation should generally communicate:

* The issue
* Relevant facts
* Expected standard
* Required improvement
* Consequences of further issues where appropriate
* Review period
* Support available
* Duration of the warning where applicable

Warnings should be issued consistently with organizational procedure.

---

# 20. Serious Misconduct

Certain conduct may require immediate escalation.

Examples may include:

* Serious dishonesty
* Fraud
* Serious threats
* Significant confidentiality breaches
* Serious harassment
* Violence
* Deliberate destruction of company property
* Serious safety violations

The organization should not automatically assume that an allegation constitutes serious misconduct. The relevant facts should be established through the appropriate process.

---

# 21. Suspension or Administrative Leave

Where appropriate and permitted, an employee may be temporarily removed from normal duties while a serious matter is reviewed.

Such action should not automatically be treated as proof of wrongdoing.

Before taking such action, the organization should consider:

* Seriousness of the concern
* Risk to employees
* Risk to evidence
* Business continuity
* Workplace safety
* Applicable requirements

Specialist HR or legal advice may be appropriate.

---

# 24. Raising a Grievance

An employee should be able to raise a grievance through an appropriate channel.

The grievance should, where possible, include:

* Nature of the concern
* Relevant dates
* People involved
* Relevant documentation
* Previous steps taken
* Desired resolution where appropriate

Employees should not be required to provide information that is unnecessary for handling the concern.

---

# 25. Grievance Investigation

Depending on the nature of the grievance, HR may:

* Review relevant documentation
* Speak with the employee
* Interview relevant individuals
* Review applicable policies
* Examine workplace records
* Assess management practices
* Identify potential solutions

The scope of the investigation should be proportionate to the issue.

---

# 26. Grievance Outcome

After reviewing the available information, the organization may:

* Uphold the grievance
* Partially uphold the grievance
* Not uphold the grievance
* Resolve the issue through another appropriate intervention
* Recommend management action
* Recommend policy or process changes

The outcome should be communicated appropriately.

---

# 27. Appeals

Where the organization provides an appeal process, employees should be informed of:

* Whether an appeal is available
* Grounds for appeal
* Deadline for submitting an appeal
* Person or body responsible for reviewing it
* Expected process
* Possible outcomes

An appeal should ideally be reviewed by someone who was not responsible for the original decision where practical and appropriate.

---

# 28. Mediation

Mediation may be appropriate where the primary issue involves workplace conflict between individuals and there is a reasonable opportunity for resolution.

Mediation may help employees:

* Understand different perspectives
* Identify underlying concerns
* Agree practical solutions
* Re-establish professional working relationships

Mediation is not appropriate for every type of case, particularly where formal investigation or other action is required.

---

# 29. Retaliation

Retaliation against an employee for:

* Raising a legitimate grievance
* Participating appropriately in an investigation
* Providing relevant information
* Exercising a protected workplace right

is prohibited to the extent required by applicable law and organizational policy.

Retaliation concerns should be escalated promptly.

---

# 30. Confidentiality

Disciplinary and grievance matters should be treated confidentially.

Information should be shared only with individuals who have a legitimate need to know.

However, confidentiality does not guarantee absolute secrecy where information must be disclosed for:

* A fair investigation
* Management decision-making
* Legal requirements
* Regulatory requirements
* Appropriate employee support

---

# 31. Documentation

* Relevant correspondence
* Employee responses
* Witness information
* Findings
* Decisions
* Warnings
* Appeal records
* Closure documentation

Records should be stored securely and retained according to the organization's approved retention requirements.

# 34. HRBP Role

The HR Business Partner plays a central role in employee relations.

The HRBP may:

* Receive or assess employee concerns
* Advise managers
* Conduct or coordinate investigations
* Review documentation
* Coach managers
* Facilitate difficult conversations
* Support grievance resolution
* Recommend appropriate actions
* Monitor recurring issues
* Identify organizational risks
* Maintain appropriate confidentiality
* Escalate matters requiring specialist advice

The HRBP should remain objective and distinguish evidence from allegations, assumptions, and personal opinions.

# 35. Manager Role

Managers are expected to:

* Address concerns promptly
* Communicate expectations clearly
* Document relevant issues
* Avoid retaliation
* Treat employees respectfully
* Apply policies consistently
* Escalate serious matters
* Cooperate with investigations
* Seek HR advice when necessary

Managers should not conduct informal investigations that could compromise a formal HR process.

# 38. Employee Relations Analytics

HR can analyze employee relations data to identify organizational patterns.

Example:

| Case Type          | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 |
| ------------------ | -: | -: | -: | -: |
| Grievances         |  8 | 11 |  7 |  6 |
| Disciplinary Cases |  5 |  7 |  6 |  4 |
| Workplace Conflict |  6 |  8 |  5 |  4 |
| Attendance Cases   |  9 | 12 | 10 |  7 |

Analytics should be used to identify trends rather than label individual employees or managers.

Possible questions include:

* Are cases concentrated in one department?
* Are certain policy areas generating repeated concerns?
* Are cases increasing or declining?
* Are similar cases being handled consistently?
* Is there a recurring management issue?
* Are employee relations concerns associated with engagement or turnover trends?

# 41. Root-Cause Analysis

When recurring employee relations issues appear, the HRBP should investigate the underlying factors.

Possible contributing factors include:

* Unclear expectations
* Poor communication
* Inconsistent management
* Workload
* Inadequate training
* Policy gaps
* Organizational change
* Team conflict
* Role ambiguity
* Process failures

The objective is to address recurring causes rather than simply process individual cases.

# 42. Investigation Quality Checklist

Before closing an investigation, HR should consider whether:

* The issue was clearly defined
* Relevant evidence was reviewed
* Appropriate people were interviewed
* The employee had an opportunity to respond
* Conflicting information was considered
* Findings were documented
* Confidentiality was maintained
* Appropriate decision-makers were involved
* The outcome was communicated
* Follow-up action was identified where necessary
