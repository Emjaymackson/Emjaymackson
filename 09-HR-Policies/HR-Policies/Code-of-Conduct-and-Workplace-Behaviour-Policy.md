# Code of Conduct and Workplace Behaviour Policy

## 1. Purpose

The Code of Conduct and Workplace Behaviour Policy establishes the standards of professional conduct expected from employees and provides guidance on appropriate behaviour in the workplace.

The policy is designed to promote:

* Professionalism
* Integrity
* Respect
* Accountability
* Ethical behaviour
* A safe and inclusive workplace
* Protection of organizational interests
* Positive employee relationships

This is a **fictionalized portfolio policy**. It should be reviewed and adapted to the organization's employment contracts, applicable laws, regulatory requirements, and professional legal advice before implementation.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to employees and, where relevant, other individuals representing or working with the organization.

Depending on organizational requirements, the principles may also apply to:

* Contractors
* Consultants
* Temporary workers
* Interns
* Vendors
* Partners
* Other workplace representatives

The organization should clearly define the categories to which each requirement applies.

---

# 3. Code of Conduct Principles

Employees are expected to conduct themselves according to the following principles:

### Integrity

Employees should act honestly and responsibly.

### Respect

Employees should treat colleagues, customers, and other stakeholders with dignity and professionalism.

### Accountability

Employees should take responsibility for their decisions, actions, and work.

### Confidentiality

Employees should protect confidential organizational and employee information.

### Fairness

Employees should avoid unfair treatment, discrimination, harassment, or inappropriate bias.

### Professionalism

Employees should maintain appropriate standards of behaviour and communication.

### Compliance

Employees should comply with applicable organizational policies and legitimate workplace requirements.

---

# 4. Professional Behaviour

Employees are expected to:

* Treat others respectfully
* Communicate professionally
* Perform their responsibilities diligently
* Follow reasonable workplace instructions
* Maintain appropriate workplace boundaries
* Protect organizational resources
* Raise concerns through appropriate channels
* Cooperate with legitimate workplace processes
* Maintain confidentiality
* Represent the organization responsibly

---

# 5. Respectful Workplace

The organization expects employees to contribute to a workplace where people can work together professionally.

Employees should avoid:

* Insults
* Threats
* Intimidation
* Humiliation
* Bullying
* Harassment
* Discriminatory conduct
* Deliberate exclusion
* Aggressive communication
* Abusive language

Disagreements are permitted, but they should be handled professionally.

---

# 6. Workplace Harassment

Harassment may include unwelcome conduct that violates workplace standards or creates an intimidating, hostile, humiliating, or offensive environment.

Examples may include:

* Repeated insulting behaviour
* Threatening conduct
* Unwelcome sexual conduct
* Offensive comments
* Intimidation
* Humiliation
* Repeated inappropriate messages
* Unwanted physical contact

Each allegation should be assessed based on the facts and circumstances.

---

# 7. Sexual Harassment

Sexual harassment is prohibited.

Potential examples include:

* Unwelcome sexual comments
* Unwanted sexual advances
* Sexual jokes directed at an employee
* Unwanted sexual messages
* Requests for sexual favours
* Display of inappropriate sexual material
* Unwelcome physical contact
* Conduct that creates an inappropriate sexualized workplace environment

Employees should have access to clear reporting channels for such concerns.

---

# 8. Bullying

Bullying may involve repeated inappropriate behaviour that causes or is reasonably capable of causing harm, intimidation, humiliation, or unreasonable distress.

Examples may include:

* Repeated public humiliation
* Persistent insults
* Threatening behaviour
* Deliberate exclusion
* Abuse of authority
* Repeated unreasonable conduct

Legitimate management activities such as performance feedback, work allocation, accountability, and disciplinary processes should not automatically be treated as bullying.

Managers must nevertheless conduct these activities professionally and appropriately.

---

# 9. Discrimination

Employees should not be subjected to unfair treatment on prohibited or otherwise protected grounds under applicable law.

The organization should maintain fair employment practices across areas such as:

* Recruitment
* Promotion
* Compensation
* Training
* Performance management
* Work allocation
* Discipline
* Termination

Managers should seek HR guidance when dealing with potentially sensitive employment decisions.

---

# 10. Communication Standards

Workplace communication should be:

* Professional
* Respectful
* Accurate
* Appropriate to the audience
* Relevant to the business purpose

This applies to:

* Email
* Meetings
* Messaging platforms
* Phone calls
* Internal communication
* Customer communication
* Written correspondence
* Digital collaboration platforms

---

# 11. Digital Communication

Employees should maintain professional conduct when using workplace communication platforms.

Employees should not use company communication systems to:

* Harass others
* Threaten colleagues
* Share confidential information improperly
* Send offensive material
* Impersonate others
* Deliberately disrupt workplace communication
* Conduct unauthorized activities

Digital communication may form part of an employee relations investigation where relevant and lawfully obtained.

---

# 12. Social Media

Employees should exercise appropriate judgment when using social media.

Employees should not:

* Disclose confidential company information
* Share protected employee information
* Misrepresent themselves as authorized organizational spokespersons
* Publish confidential customer information
* Use company resources improperly
* Engage in unlawful conduct using organizational accounts

Employees may maintain personal social media accounts, but they remain responsible for complying with applicable organizational policies and legal requirements.

---

# 13. Confidentiality

Employees may have access to confidential information.

Examples include:

* Employee records
* Customer records
* Financial information
* Business strategies
* Commercial information
* Contracts
* Passwords
* Internal reports
* Proprietary information

Employees must only access, use, or disclose information for legitimate purposes and in accordance with organizational requirements.

---

# 14. Conflict of Interest

Employees should avoid situations where personal interests conflict with organizational responsibilities.

Potential conflicts may involve:

* Personal financial interests
* Outside employment
* Business ownership
* Supplier relationships
* Customer relationships
* Personal relationships affecting business decisions
* Gifts or benefits
* Use of organizational opportunities for personal gain

Employees should disclose potential conflicts through the appropriate process.

---

# 15. Outside Employment and Business Activities

Employees who engage in outside employment or business activities should ensure that these activities:

* Do not improperly interfere with their duties
* Do not create an unmanaged conflict of interest
* Do not misuse organizational resources
* Do not involve unauthorized use of confidential information
* Comply with contractual and organizational requirements

Where disclosure or approval is required, employees should follow the applicable process.

---

# 16. Gifts and Hospitality

Employees should exercise appropriate judgment when offering or receiving gifts or hospitality in connection with their work.

Factors to consider include:

* Value
* Frequency
* Business purpose
* Relationship between the parties
* Potential influence on decision-making
* Applicable organizational rules

Employees should not accept benefits that could improperly influence business decisions.

---

# 17. Fraud and Dishonesty

The organization does not tolerate fraudulent or dishonest conduct.

Examples may include:

* Falsifying records
* Misappropriating company resources
* Deliberate financial deception
* Falsifying expenses
* Misrepresenting qualifications
* Falsifying attendance
* Unauthorized manipulation of company information

Concerns should be reported through appropriate channels.

---

# 18. Company Property

Employees are responsible for protecting organizational property entrusted to them.

This may include:

* Computers
* Mobile devices
* Vehicles
* Equipment
* Documents
* Access cards
* Software
* Financial resources
* Intellectual property

Company property should be used responsibly and for authorized purposes.

---

# 19. Information Security

Employees should follow organizational information security requirements.

Expected practices may include:

* Protecting passwords
* Using approved systems
* Avoiding unauthorized software
* Reporting suspicious activity
* Protecting devices
* Avoiding unauthorized data sharing
* Following access-control requirements

Employees should report suspected security incidents promptly.

---

# 20. Workplace Safety

Employees are expected to contribute to a safe working environment.

Employees should:

* Follow safety procedures
* Report hazards
* Use equipment appropriately
* Report workplace incidents
* Follow emergency procedures
* Avoid intentionally unsafe behaviour

Managers are responsible for addressing safety concerns through appropriate channels.

---

# 21. Substance Misuse

Employees should not report to work impaired in a way that creates a safety, performance, or workplace risk.

Organizational requirements relating to alcohol, drugs, medication, or other substances should be communicated clearly and applied in accordance with applicable requirements.

Where an employee requires support, the organization should provide appropriate avenues for assistance where available.

---

# 22. Workplace Relationships

Employees may develop personal relationships with colleagues.

However, employees should maintain professional boundaries and avoid situations that create:

* Conflicts of interest
* Favouritism
* Abuse of authority
* Confidentiality concerns
* Workplace disruption

Additional disclosure requirements may apply where a relationship involves a direct reporting line or decision-making authority.

---

# 23. Manager Conduct

Managers have additional responsibilities because of their authority.

Managers should:

* Treat employees fairly
* Communicate expectations clearly
* Provide constructive feedback
* Avoid intimidation
* Avoid retaliation
* Maintain appropriate confidentiality
* Apply policies consistently
* Document significant employee relations matters
* Seek HR advice where necessary

Managers must not use organizational authority for personal benefit or inappropriate retaliation.

---

# 24. Abuse of Authority

Employees should not misuse their organizational position.

Examples may include:

* Threatening employees with inappropriate consequences
* Requesting personal favours because of one's position
* Using authority for personal financial gain
* Deliberately withholding legitimate workplace opportunities
* Retaliating against an employee for raising a legitimate concern

Concerns should be escalated through appropriate channels.

---

# 25. Workplace Retaliation

Retaliation against an employee for raising a legitimate concern, participating appropriately in an investigation, or exercising a protected workplace right is prohibited to the extent required by applicable law and organizational policy.

Retaliation concerns should be reported promptly.

---

# 26. Raising Concerns

Employees should have multiple appropriate channels for raising concerns where practical.

Possible channels include:

* Immediate manager
* HR
* Senior management
* Designated ethics or compliance channel
* Other approved reporting mechanisms

Employees should be informed of the appropriate route for different types of concerns.

---

# 27. Reporting Process

A general concern-reporting process may follow:

```text id="f0f6h7"
Concern Identified
        ↓
Concern Reported
        ↓
Initial HR / Management Review
        ↓
Risk Assessment
        ↓
Investigation Where Required
        ↓
Findings
        ↓
Appropriate Action
        ↓
Communication / Closure
        ↓
Follow-Up Where Necessary
```

Not every concern requires a formal investigation. The appropriate response should depend on the nature and seriousness of the issue.

---

# 28. Investigation Principles

Where an investigation is required, the organization should aim to:

* Establish relevant facts
* Identify appropriate witnesses
* Review relevant documentation
* Maintain appropriate confidentiality
* Give involved employees an opportunity to provide information
* Avoid prejudging the outcome
* Document material findings
* Take appropriate action based on the findings

Investigations should be handled by appropriately trained individuals.

Specialist support may be appropriate for complex or high-risk matters.

---

# 29. Confidentiality During Investigations

Information relating to workplace investigations should be shared only with individuals who have a legitimate need to know.

Employees participating in investigations should be reminded to respect confidentiality where appropriate.

Confidentiality does not necessarily mean absolute secrecy, particularly where disclosure is required for a fair investigation or by applicable law.

---

# 30. False or Malicious Reports

Employees are encouraged to raise legitimate concerns.

Knowingly making false allegations or deliberately providing misleading information may itself constitute a workplace conduct issue.

However, a concern should not be treated as malicious simply because it cannot be substantiated.

This distinction is important for maintaining employee trust in reporting processes.

---

# 31. Performance and Conduct

Performance and conduct should be assessed separately where appropriate.

Examples:

**Performance issue:**

An employee repeatedly fails to meet agreed work objectives.

**Conduct issue:**

An employee deliberately violates a workplace requirement.

Some situations may involve both performance and conduct considerations and should be assessed based on the specific facts.

---

# 32. Dress and Professional Appearance

Where the organization has workplace appearance requirements, employees should follow reasonable standards appropriate to:

* Job responsibilities
* Customer interaction
* Safety requirements
* Organizational environment
* Cultural considerations

Requirements should be communicated clearly and applied consistently.

---

# 33. Customer and External Stakeholder Conduct

Employees representing the organization externally should:

* Communicate professionally
* Protect confidential information
* Treat customers respectfully
* Avoid inappropriate commitments
* Follow approval procedures
* Escalate serious customer concerns

Employees should not make unauthorized statements on behalf of the organization.

---

# 34. Remote Workplace Behaviour

The Code of Conduct also applies to approved remote work environments.

Employees should maintain:

* Professional communication
* Appropriate meeting behaviour
* Confidentiality
* Information security
* Respectful conduct
* Appropriate availability

Remote work does not remove workplace conduct expectations.

---

# 35. Meetings

Employees should contribute professionally during meetings.

Expected behaviours include:

* Arriving prepared
* Respecting meeting schedules
* Allowing others to speak
* Avoiding disruptive behaviour
* Staying relevant to the discussion
* Following agreed actions
* Respecting confidentiality

Managers should create an environment where employees can contribute appropriately.

---

# 36. Professional Disagreement

Employees may disagree with decisions, ideas, or approaches.

Professional disagreement should:

* Focus on the issue
* Use evidence where appropriate
* Avoid personal attacks
* Respect different viewpoints
* Follow appropriate escalation channels
* Support the final decision once appropriately made

Disagreement itself is not misconduct.

---

# 37. Non-Compliance

Failure to comply with the Code of Conduct may result in appropriate management action.

Depending on the circumstances, the organization may use:

* Coaching
* Counselling
* Clarification of expectations
* Corrective action
* Formal disciplinary procedures
* Other appropriate interventions

Any formal disciplinary action should follow the applicable disciplinary process.

---

# 38. Employee Responsibilities

Employees are expected to:

* Read and understand applicable workplace policies
* Maintain professional conduct
* Treat colleagues respectfully
* Protect confidential information
* Follow legitimate workplace requirements
* Report serious concerns appropriately
* Cooperate with legitimate investigations
* Protect organizational resources
* Avoid conflicts of interest
* Maintain appropriate professional boundaries

---

# 39. Manager Responsibilities

Managers should:

* Model expected behaviour
* Communicate workplace standards
* Address concerns promptly
* Avoid favoritism
* Apply policies consistently
* Document significant issues
* Protect confidentiality
* Escalate serious concerns
* Seek HR guidance
* Support a respectful workplace

---

# 40. HR Responsibilities

HR should:

* Maintain the policy
* Advise managers
* Support employees
* Review conduct concerns
* Coordinate investigations where appropriate
* Monitor recurring conduct issues
* Maintain appropriate records
* Support consistent policy application
* Recommend improvements

---

# 41. HRBP Role

The HR Business Partner plays an important role in connecting workplace behaviour standards with organizational culture.

The HRBP may:

* Advise leadership
* Coach managers
* Support employee relations
* Review workplace behaviour trends
* Identify culture risks
* Support investigations
* Recommend interventions
* Review policy effectiveness
* Support organizational change
* Use employee feedback to identify behavioural patterns

The HRBP should remain objective when handling employee relations matters and distinguish verified facts from allegations or assumptions.

---

# 42. Conduct Risk Indicators

HR may monitor patterns such as:

* Repeated employee complaints
* Increased grievances
* Harassment allegations
* Workplace conflict
* Repeated disciplinary cases
* Manager conduct concerns
* Retaliation complaints
* High turnover within a particular team
* Employee engagement concerns
* Repeated policy violations

These indicators should trigger appropriate review rather than automatic conclusions about individuals or teams.
